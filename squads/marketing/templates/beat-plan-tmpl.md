## 2. Candidate beats

A **beat** is a territory the brand covers continuously and credibly. A topic list produces
disconnected pieces; beats accumulate authority, so each piece is worth more because of the ones
around it.

| Beat | Territory (one sentence) | Our genuine claim to it | CEPs it serves | Who covers it credibly today | What our coverage adds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| | | expertise / proprietary data / operating vantage point / customer access | | | |

**"We are interested in it" is not a claim.** Neither is "it ranks". A beat where the claim is
weak produces content indistinguishable from everyone else's, and it consumes the capacity that
a defensible beat needed.

### Defensibility test, per candidate

- [ ] Could a competitor publish this identically tomorrow? If yes → the claim is weak.
- [ ] Does the brand have access to something — data, operations, customers — that the territory
      requires?
- [ ] Does the beat serve at least one CEP from §1, named explicitly?
- [ ] Can we sustain it for the commitment horizon in §5, at the capacity in §6?

## 7. Distribution model

Distribution is most of the work, and production and distribution effort are routinely inverted.

| Route class | Routes used | Decay profile | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| OWNED — site, archive, newsletter, community | | slow decay, compounds, ours | |
| EARNED — coverage, citation, inclusion by others | | unpredictable, high value, not controllable | |
| PAID — promotion and amplification | | fast, stops when the money stops; **sizing is `@marketing:demand-lead`** | |

- **Waves planned per piece:** at least a second and third, with dates. Most content is
  distributed once at publication and then abandoned.
- **Repurposing rule:** same idea, second form, *different* audience = new distribution, worth
  doing. Same idea, recut, same audience = noise.
- **Effort split, production vs distribution:** if production exceeds distribution, flag the
  inversion here and say what would have to change.

## 10. Standards this plan operates under

Reference the current editorial standard (`*editorial-standards`) rather than restating it.

- **Sourcing rule in force:** every factual claim, statistic and quotation is sourced before
  publication. A remembered statistic is not a source. Content citing the marketing literature
  names the publication and the year rather than paraphrasing from memory. Unsourced claims are
  **cut, not softened**.
- **Corrections rule in force:** corrections are published and dated, never silently edited.
- **AI-assisted drafting rule in force:** what may be drafted with assistance, what must be
  verified by a human before publication, what is disclosed.
- **Approver for this plan:** one named person.
