# Brand Growth Evidence Checklist

**Checklist ID:** MKT-CL-001
**Referenced by:** `@marketing:brand-lead` (Salience)
**Applies to:** a brand specification, a salience brief, a rebrand proposal, or any brand plan
about to consume budget
**Purpose:** apply the test the method actually applies — is the growth mechanism penetration,
is the denominator the category, and is every asset claim measured rather than believed.

**Method source.** Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow: What Marketers Don't Know* (Oxford University
Press, 2010). Category entry points: Jenni Romaniuk and Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow Part 2*
(2016). The fame-and-uniqueness grid: Jenni Romaniuk, *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (2018).
These are named separately because the concepts come from different works, and collapsing them
into one citation makes the method unauditable.

## A. Gate — denominator and mechanism

*Any FAIL here blocks approval. These are not weighted items.*

- [ ] **A.1** The category is defined from the buyer's point of view, and the definition is
  taken from `@products:positioning-lead` rather than invented in the brand plan.
- [ ] **A.2** The number of *category buyers* is stated with a named source. Market size in
  currency is not a denominator; a count of buyers is.
- [ ] **A.3** Brand health readings are expressed as a share of category buyers, not as a share
  of the existing customer list. *(A plan measured against current customers recommends loyalty
  work by construction — the acquirable population is not in the data.)*
- [ ] **A.4** The growth mechanism is stated explicitly: more buyers, more purchases per buyer,
  or more revenue per purchase. "Grow the brand" is not a mechanism.
- [ ] **A.5** If the mechanism is anything other than penetration, the plan presents evidence
  that this brand is an exception to double jeopardy — not a conviction that it is.

## B. Buyer base and diagnosis

- [ ] **B.1** Loyalty readings are compared against the double-jeopardy expectation for a brand
  of this share before any loyalty investment is proposed.
- [ ] **B.2** Where loyalty sits at the expectation, the plan says so explicitly and does not
  fund a fix. *(Spending on a number that is not underperforming is the most expensive form of
  being busy.)*
- [ ] **B.3** The buyer base shape is reported from this brand's own purchase distribution, not
  from a remembered concentration ratio. The 80/20 figure is folklore unless this brand's data
  produces it.
- [ ] **B.4** Duplication of purchase with competing brands is reported, and compared against
  those brands' shares.
- [ ] **B.5** Any segmentation claim that this brand's buyers are fundamentally different people
  is tested against purchase data, and marked UNSUPPORTED where the data does not show it.
- [ ] **B.6** Light and non-buyers are present in the plan as a population to be reached, not
  optimised out because each individually buys little.

## C. Mental availability

- [ ] **C.1** A category entry point map exists, elicited across the cue set (Why, When, Where,
  With whom, While doing what, With what, How feeling) rather than from a topic list.
- [ ] **C.2** Each CEP row carries a retrieval reading marked SOURCED or ESTIMATED. A map where
  every row is estimated is labelled a hypothesis in the document itself.
- [ ] **C.3** Two to four CEPs are prioritised for building, with the prioritisation rule
  (frequency × retrieval gap) visible rather than asserted.
- [ ] **C.4** The plan does not build depth on the single entry point already owned. *(That
  reinforces the current ceiling — the entry point's own frequency.)*
- [ ] **C.5** Prompted brand awareness is not presented as evidence of mental availability.
  Recognition of a name and retrieval in a buying situation are different measurements, and the
  first routinely looks healthy while the second is weak.
- [ ] **C.6** Advertising is assessed against the branding and attention conditions — noticed,
  correctly attributed, connected to a buying situation — and not on persuasiveness alone.
- [ ] **C.7** For each proposed execution the plan answers: from this alone, without the logo,
  would a category buyer name this brand?

## D. Distinctive assets

- [ ] **D.1** Every asset carries a **measured** fame figure and a **measured** uniqueness
  figure, or is explicitly marked UNTESTED. No asset is graded from intuition.
- [ ] **D.2** Fame was tested with the asset shown *without the brand name*. A test that shows
  the name measures recognition of the name, not of the asset.
- [ ] **D.3** Uniqueness is reported separately from fame, not folded into a single "asset
  strength" score. *(High fame with low uniqueness is the worst case and is invisible in a
  combined score — it advertises the category leader.)*
- [ ] **D.4** Each asset is placed on the four-box grid with its change consequence stated.
- [ ] **D.5** Every UNTESTED asset has its test specified and scheduled, not deferred
  indefinitely.
- [ ] **D.6** No asset change is proposed without a rebrand-risk record naming the fame figure
  being reset and the years of consistent exposure that produced it.
- [ ] **D.7** Where a change is proposed, the claimed benefit has evidence. "It feels dated" is
  a preference. Measured mis-identification, measured category confusion, a legal constraint or
  a genuine category shift are evidence.
- [ ] **D.8** Distinctiveness and differentiation are not used interchangeably anywhere in the
  document.

## E. Physical availability

- [ ] **E.1** The buying path is mapped step by step, from the moment of need to completed
  purchase.
- [ ] **E.2** Each friction is classified — absence, obscurity, barrier, drag — rather than
  described loosely.
- [ ] **E.3** Removals are ranked by buyers affected × drop-off, and where drop-off is not
  instrumented that instrumentation is named as the first action.
- [ ] **E.4** The plan does not propose building retrieval for a product a buyer still cannot
  easily find or complete a purchase of.

## F. Reach

- [ ] **F.1** Coverage is stated as a share of category buyers, not as impressions, reach curves
  or spend.
- [ ] **F.2** Continuity is stated, and any dark periods are named with the buying that occurs
  in them.
- [ ] **F.3** Where targeting narrows below the category, the plan carries purchase-data
  evidence that the excluded buyers do not buy the category — not a persona document.
- [ ] **F.4** Where the narrowing is driven by budget, it is recorded as a budget constraint and
  routed to `@marketing:demand-lead`, not converted into a strategic claim.

## G. Attribution and evidence integrity

- [ ] **G.1** Every empirical claim names its source: *How Brands Grow* (2010), *How Brands Grow
  Part 2* (2016), *Building Distinctive Brand Assets* (2018), or this brand's own data.
- [ ] **G.2** Concepts from the later Ehrenberg-Bass works are attributed to those works and not
  folded into the 2010 book.
- [ ] **G.3** Every quoted figure is marked SOURCED or UNVERIFIED. A figure recalled from memory
  and presented as sourced is a **critical** defect — it survives review by looking correct.
- [ ] **G.4** No empirical generalisation is stated as a guarantee for this brand. It is a
  pattern observed across categories; this brand's own data may deviate, and the plan says so.
- [ ] **G.5** No UNVERIFIED figure is load-bearing for a decision. It may inform a hypothesis.

## H. Boundary

- [ ] **H.1** The plan contains no budget figure, no brand-vs-activation split and no share of
  voice position. Those are `@marketing:demand-lead`.
- [ ] **H.2** The plan does not define the market category, the competitive alternatives or the
  segment. Those are `@products:positioning-lead` and are consumed here.
- [ ] **H.3** The plan does not design the measurement instrument. It specifies constructs and
  hands them to `@marketing:analytics-lead`.
- [ ] **H.4** The plan contains no price or packaging decision (`@products:pricing-strategist`),
  no implementation (`@dev`), no test plan (`@qa`), no release step (`@devops`) and no story
  (`@sm`).

## I. Capture

- [ ] **I.1** Written to the repository with an owner and a review date, not left in a
  transcript.
- [ ] **I.2** Success is expressed as a tracking movement with a magnitude, an instrument and a
  period — not as a campaign deliverable.
- [ ] **I.3** At least one falsifying observation is named: what would tell us this plan failed,
  and by when.

---

## Verdict

**Gate:** any FAIL in Section A → `BLOCKED`, regardless of score below.

**Score:** checked items ÷ (total applicable items) across B–I.

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| `APPROVED` | Section A clean, score ≥ 90%, no FAIL in D.1, D.6 or G.3 |
| `CONCERNS` | Section A clean, score 75–89%, or a single FAIL in a non-critical item |
| `REWORK` | Score below 75%, or any FAIL in D.1 (assets graded by intuition), D.6 (asset change with no risk record) or G.3 (unsourced figure presented as sourced) |
| `BLOCKED` | Any FAIL in Section A |

**Critical items regardless of score:** A.1–A.5, D.1, D.6, G.3.

## Priority fix order

1. **Section A** — denominator and mechanism. Everything downstream inherits these.
2. **G.3** — unsourced figures presented as sourced. An attribution defect makes the whole plan
   unauditable, so it is fixed before anything is measured.
3. **D.1 / D.6** — asset claims and asset changes. These destroy accumulated equity and the
   destruction is irreversible on any useful timescale.
4. **C** — mental availability. The usual binding constraint.
5. **E / F** — availability and reach.
6. **H / I** — boundary and capture.
