# Effect Window & Short-Termism Checklist

**Checklist ID:** MKT-CL-002
**Referenced by:** `@marketing:demand-lead` (Cadence)
**Applies to:** any readout that concludes something worked or did not, any budget cut proposal,
any efficiency ranking, and any demand plan before budget lock
**Purpose:** apply the test the method actually applies — was the effect judged over a window
that could contain it, and is the budget drifting toward what is measurable rather than toward
what produces value.

**Method source.** Les Binet and Peter Field, *The Long and the Short of It* (IPA, 2013),
analysing the IPA Databank. Later work named separately where used: *Media in Focus* (IPA, 2017)
and the authors' B2B-specific analysis published with the LinkedIn B2B Institute (2019).

## A. Gate — the window

*Any FAIL here blocks the conclusion, regardless of the score below.*

- [ ] **A.1** Every activity being judged is classified: brand building, sales activation, or
  hybrid — classified by **optimisation target**, not by label.
- [ ] **A.2** For each activity the expected effect shape is stated: when the effect begins,
  when it peaks, how it decays.
- [ ] **A.3** The minimum window that could contain the effect is stated, alongside the window
  actually used.
- [ ] **A.4** Where the window used is shorter than the effect, the readout is labelled
  **UNINTERPRETABLE** — not "inconclusive", not "no measurable effect", not "negative so far".
- [ ] **A.5** No too-short readout has been converted into a directional conclusion anywhere in
  the artifact, including in a summary line, an executive slide or a footnote.
- [ ] **A.6** The language distinguishes "effect not measured" from "no effect". The second is a
  claim the data did not make.
- [ ] **A.7** Where an effect was never instrumented at all, it is recorded as NOT MEASURED and
  is not implied to be zero. Absence of measurement is not evidence of absence.

## B. The split

- [ ] **B.1** The brand-vs-activation split is stated explicitly. *(A plan with no stated split
  has one anyway — set by whatever was easiest to justify last budget round, and now invisible
  and therefore unreviewable.)*
- [ ] **B.2** The **actual** split is reported line by line, independent of the split the plan
  claims, and the divergence is explained.
- [ ] **B.3** Hybrid lines are identified, apportioned by a recorded judgement, and not silently
  assigned whole to one side.
- [ ] **B.4** Any line labelled brand building but optimised for immediate response is flagged.
  It behaves as activation regardless of its label.
- [ ] **B.5** The recommended split is a **range with a mechanism**, not a point estimate.
- [ ] **B.6** Each adjustment away from the prior states a direction, a reason and an evidence
  class.
- [ ] **B.7** A physical availability constraint, if present, is treated as a prerequisite and
  not as a split adjustment. Neither mechanism pays back through a blocked buying path.

## C. Share of voice

- [ ] **C.1** Share of market and share of voice are both stated, with sources.
- [ ] **C.2** The measurement basis is named — spend, impressions or reach — and comparisons stay
  inside one basis.
- [ ] **C.3** Excess share of voice is computed rather than asserted.
- [ ] **C.4** The competitor-increase scenario is modelled explicitly. *(A flat budget while
  competitors increase is a cut that nobody logged as one.)*
- [ ] **C.5** Any share outcome derived from the excess-share-of-voice relationship is presented
  as a **range with named assumptions**, and the artifact states what the model cannot know:
  creative quality, competitor creative, category shocks, distribution changes.
- [ ] **C.6** The relationship is used to size an argument between options, not to promise an
  outcome.

## D. Short-termism / the efficiency trap

Six signature tests. Record the answer to each; three or more affirmatives indicates the trap.

- [ ] **D.1** Has the share of budget going to directly-attributable channels risen over the
  last three planning cycles? *(answer recorded, with data)*
- [ ] **D.2** Has cost per acquisition risen while total demand stayed flat or fell?
- [ ] **D.3** Are any brand activities being judged on windows of a quarter or less?
- [ ] **D.4** Is brand spend the first line proposed when a cut is required?
- [ ] **D.5** Are the metrics presented to leadership all short-window response metrics?
- [ ] **D.6** Has price elasticity worsened or discounting deepened over two or more years?
- [ ] **D.7** The verdict is recorded with the evidence per test, and any test that could not be
  answered from available data is marked as unanswerable rather than assumed negative.
- [ ] **D.8** Rising acquisition cost, where present, is not narrated as "the market got harder"
  without first testing "we stopped replenishing demand".

## E. Attribution and figure integrity — GATE

*Any FAIL here is CRITICAL.*

- [ ] **E.1** Every ratio, coefficient and effect size names the publication it comes from:
  Binet and Field (IPA, 2013), *Media in Focus* (IPA, 2017), or the authors' LinkedIn B2B
  Institute work (2019).
- [ ] **E.2** Every such figure was **read from the publication in preparing this artifact**, and
  the section read is recorded. A figure that was not read is marked `UNVERIFIED`.
- [ ] **E.3** No figure recalled from memory is presented as sourced. This is the defect that
  passes review by looking correct.
- [ ] **E.4** No published ratio is presented as a constant or as this business's correct answer.
  They are category averages across a case databank, with dispersion.
- [ ] **E.5** The dispersion reported alongside a figure is carried into the artifact, not
  dropped.
- [ ] **E.6** No decision in the artifact rests on an UNVERIFIED figure alone.
- [ ] **E.7** Every figure carries an evidence class: `OWN DATA`, `DATABANK PRIOR`, `ANALOGUE`
  or `JUDGEMENT`.

## F. Metrics and comparison hygiene

- [ ] **F.1** Metrics are assigned to horizons — short, medium, long — with the window over which
  each is interpretable and the window over which it is noise.
- [ ] **F.2** **No short-horizon and long-horizon metrics appear in the same efficiency ranking.**
  Structurally, the short-horizon activity wins that ranking regardless of value produced.
- [ ] **F.3** Instrumentability of each metric is confirmed by `@marketing:analytics-lead`, not
  assumed by this artifact.
- [ ] **F.4** Any metric marked NOT FEASIBLE is recorded as an open measurement gap and is **not**
  quietly replaced with an easier proxy. A substitution escalates to `@marketing-chief`.

## G. Cuts

- [ ] **G.1** Any proposed cut states what improves immediately, in its strongest form.
- [ ] **G.2** It states what degrades and **when each degradation becomes visible** — weeks,
  months, quarters to years.
- [ ] **G.3** It states the recovery asymmetry: rebuilding costs more and takes longer than
  maintaining.
- [ ] **G.4** It names leading indicators so the cost becomes visible early enough to act.
- [ ] **G.5** It is framed as a trade, not as a veto. A business that needs cash may correctly
  take it.
- [ ] **G.6** It is not presented as free. Concealing the second half of the trade is the defect,
  not the cut itself.

## H. Falsification and boundary

- [ ] **H.1** At least one falsifying observation is named, with a deadline.
- [ ] **H.2** What cannot currently be proven is stated, along with what proving it would
  require and roughly cost.
- [ ] **H.3** The artifact does not decide what the brand should mean, which entry points to
  build, or which assets to use (`@marketing:brand-lead`).
- [ ] **H.4** The artifact does not claim an effect is measurable without confirmation from
  `@marketing:analytics-lead`.
- [ ] **H.5** The artifact contains no positioning decision (`@products:positioning-lead`), no
  price or packaging decision (`@products:pricing-strategist`), no epic (`@pm`), no story
  (`@sm`), no implementation (`@dev`), no test plan (`@qa`) and no push (`@devops`, exclusive).
- [ ] **H.6** Written to the repository with an owner and a review date.

## Verdict

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| `APPROVED` | Sections A and E clean; score ≥ 90% across B, C, D, F, G, H |
| `CONCERNS` | Sections A and E clean; score 75–89% |
| `REWORK` | Score below 75%, or any FAIL in B.1, B.5, F.2 or G.6 |
| `BLOCKED` | Any FAIL in Section A (window) or Section E (figure integrity) |

**Critical items regardless of score:** A.4, A.5, A.7, E.2, E.3, F.2, G.6.

## Priority fix order

1. **Section A** — the window. Every conclusion downstream inherits it, and a too-short window
   cancels working investment on an artifact rather than on evidence.
2. **Section E** — figure integrity. An unsourced coefficient makes the plan unauditable, so it
   is fixed before any number is argued about.
3. **F.2** — cross-horizon ranking. It guarantees the wrong answer by construction.
4. **B.1 / B.5** — the split, stated and ranged.
5. **G.6** — the free cut. The trade may be right; concealing its second half is not.
6. **C / D** — share of voice and drift diagnosis.
7. **H** — falsification, boundary and capture.
