# Commissioning Brief Checklist

**Checklist ID:** MKT-CL-003
**Referenced by:** `@marketing:content-lead` (Quill)
**Applies to:** any brief before a piece is commissioned, and any finished piece before it is
published
**Purpose:** apply the editorial gate — a piece is not commissioned until the reader, the job
and the encounter have specific answers, and it is not published until every claim in it is
sourced.

**Attribution, stated honestly.** This checklist rests on editorial craft practice — the brief,
the beat, the calendar, the standard, the corrections policy, the archive — applied to
marketing. **No canonical published work founds this role and none is claimed for it.** These
items are an operating stance, arguable on their merits rather than on the strength of a
citation. Where an item depends on a published method it names the squad member who carries it:
`@marketing:brand-lead` (Sharp 2010; Romaniuk and Sharp 2016; Romaniuk 2018),
`@marketing:demand-lead` (Binet and Field, IPA 2013), `@marketing:analytics-lead` (Kaushik 2009).

Mark each item [x] pass, [ ] fail, [N/A] with a written reason.

Two gates, and they behave differently:

## A. Gate 1 — commission (run before anything is written)

*Any FAIL here means the piece is not commissioned.*

- [ ] **A.1 READER** — a specific person in a specific situation. *"Marketers" is not a reader.
  "A marketing lead who has just been asked to justify brand spend to a CFO" is a reader.*
- [ ] **A.2 JOB** — what this does for that reader that they cannot get elsewhere as easily. *If
  the honest answer is that it does the same job as three existing pieces, the correct action is
  to improve one of those, not publish a fourth.*
- [ ] **A.3 BEAT** — which beat it belongs to, and how it advances that beat. A piece belonging
  to no beat is an orphan at birth.
- [ ] **A.4 ENCOUNTER** — how that reader comes across it. Name the **route**, not the channel.
  *"We will post it" is not an encounter.*
- [ ] **A.5 ONE JOB** — the piece is asked to do one job. *A piece asked to build authority,
  capture demand, support sales and rank in search will do none of them, and will afterwards be
  defended on whichever it accidentally achieved.*
- [ ] **A.6 SINGLE ARGUMENT** — the one thing the piece asserts, written in one sentence.
- [ ] **A.7 APPROVER** — one named person who decides publication and who owns drift from this
  brief.

**Verdict at Gate 1:** `COMMISSION` / `REVISE THE IDEA` / `DO NOT PUBLISH`.

## B. Fit to the plan

- [ ] **B.1** The beat exists in the current beat plan and has not been quietly reopened after
  being declined.
- [ ] **B.2** The beat serves a category entry point taken from `@marketing:brand-lead`, not a
  search-volume list. *(Search volume records what is asked, not what is worth being retrievable
  in.)*
- [ ] **B.3** The piece does not contradict the current position from
  `@products:positioning-lead`, and does not teach a frame that positioning has retired.
- [ ] **B.4** Distinctive assets that must survive into written and spoken work
  (`@marketing:brand-lead`) are respected — voice, naming conventions, recurring devices.
- [ ] **B.5** The commission fits the declared capacity and does not silently consume the
  maintenance budget.

## C. Format and shelf life

- [ ] **C.1** Format is chosen for the **job and the reader's situation**, not for the channel
  and not for the trend. *(Choosing a format because a channel rewards it means the channel has
  selected the content.)*
- [ ] **C.2** The format is evaluated on four axes: fit to the idea, fit to the reader's
  situation, production cost, and maintenance cost over the declared shelf life.
- [ ] **C.3** Maintenance cost is weighted properly. *Video and interactive formats are expensive
  to correct and are therefore poor choices in a fast-changing area, regardless of how well they
  perform on first publication.*
- [ ] **C.4** Shelf life is declared at commission: perishable, seasonal or evergreen.
- [ ] **C.5** The maintenance obligation the shelf life creates is stated, with an owner. *(An
  evergreen piece nobody maintains becomes a liability while still looking like an asset.)*
- [ ] **C.6** Rejected format alternatives are recorded with reasons.

## D. Distribution — planned at commission, never after

- [ ] **D.1** The distribution plan exists **in the brief**, not as a step after publication.
  *(Distribution planned afterwards is hope, and it is the most common reason good work has no
  effect.)*
- [ ] **D.2** Routes are mapped across owned, earned and paid, with the decay of each labelled.
- [ ] **D.3** At least a second and third wave are scheduled with dates. Most content is
  distributed once and then abandoned.
- [ ] **D.4** Repurposing decisions are explicit and pass the rule: same idea, second form,
  *different* audience is new distribution; same idea recut for the same audience is noise.
- [ ] **D.5** Every route has a named executor and a date. An unowned distribution step does not
  happen.
- [ ] **D.6** The production-vs-distribution effort split is stated. If production exceeds
  distribution, the inversion is flagged and what would have to change is named.
- [ ] **D.7** Any paid amplification is sized by `@marketing:demand-lead`, not here.

## E. Success definition

- [ ] **E.1** A success definition exists, expressed as something that could actually be
  observed.
- [ ] **E.2** It has been confirmed measurable by `@marketing:analytics-lead`, or is explicitly
  marked **NOT MEASURABLE**.
- [ ] **E.3** Where it is not measurable, no convenient proxy has been substituted. *(A piece
  judged on a metric it was never commissioned to move is being judged incorrectly; the
  substitution escalates to `@marketing-chief`.)*
- [ ] **E.4** The success definition matches the single job in A.5 — not a different job the
  piece might incidentally serve.
- [ ] **E.5** Engagement and traffic figures, if used, are not presented as evidence of the job
  being done unless the job *was* traffic.

## F. Gate 2 — publication (run before the piece goes live)

*Any FAIL here blocks publication.*

- [ ] **F.1** Every factual claim, statistic and quotation is **sourced**, with the publication
  and year named.
- [ ] **F.2** No claim rests on a remembered statistic. A remembered statistic is not a source.
- [ ] **F.3** Where the piece cites the marketing literature, it names the publication rather
  than paraphrasing from memory — and it attributes to the right work. *Sharp (2010),
  Romaniuk and Sharp (2016) and Romaniuk (2018) are three different works; Binet and Field
  (IPA, 2013) and* Media in Focus *(IPA, 2017) are two; Kaushik's 2009 book and his later
  Occam's Razor blog frameworks are not the same source.*
- [ ] **F.4** No citation, title, author, year or figure has been invented or approximated. **A
  plausible-sounding citation that cannot be checked is worse than none** — it passes review by
  looking correct and is then quoted by people who assume someone verified it.
- [ ] **F.5** Unsourced claims are **cut, not softened**. Hedging language does not convert an
  unsourced claim into an acceptable one; it conceals it.
- [ ] **F.6** Claims requiring legal or specialist review have had it.
- [ ] **F.7** AI-assisted drafting rules were followed: what was drafted with assistance has been
  verified by a human, and disclosure is as the standard requires. *(Unverified generated claims
  are the fastest route to an unsourced assertion under the brand's name.)*
- [ ] **F.8** The piece has not drifted from the brief. Where it has, the drift was an editorial
  decision taken by the named approver — not something discovered at review.

## G. Archive obligations created

- [ ] **G.1** The piece is entered in the archive record with its beat, shelf life, owner and
  next review date.
- [ ] **G.2** For an evergreen piece, the review cadence is set based on how fast the territory
  changes.
- [ ] **G.3** If the piece supersedes existing pieces, the consolidation or redirect decision is
  made now, not left as a future tidy-up.
- [ ] **G.4** Where a correction would be needed to something already published, that correction
  is scheduled — published and dated, never a silent edit.

## H. Boundary

- [ ] **H.1** The brief does not define which CEPs or assets to build
  (`@marketing:brand-lead`).
- [ ] **H.2** The brief contains no budget, split or share of voice figure
  (`@marketing:demand-lead`).
- [ ] **H.3** The brief does not assert what content performance proves
  (`@marketing:analytics-lead`).
- [ ] **H.4** The brief does not set the position, the category or the segment
  (`@products:positioning-lead`), and no price or packaging claim beyond what
  `@products:pricing-strategist` has published.
- [ ] **H.5** The brief covers no interface copy, microcopy or product UX writing
  (`@ux-design-expert`).
- [ ] **H.6** No epic (`@pm`), no story (`@sm`), no implementation (`@dev`), no test plan
  (`@qa`), no push (`@devops`, exclusive).

## Verdict

| Verdict | Condition |
|---|---|
| `COMMISSION` | Section A clean; score ≥ 85% across B–E and H |
| `REVISE THE IDEA` | Section A clean; score 70–84%, or a FAIL in D.1 or E.3 |
| `DO NOT PUBLISH` | Any FAIL in Section A |
| `BLOCKED AT PUBLICATION` | Any FAIL in Section F, regardless of everything else |

**Critical items regardless of score:** A.1, A.2, A.4, D.1, E.3, F.1, F.4, F.5.

## Priority fix order

1. **Section A** — reader, job, beat, encounter. Execution quality cannot rescue a failure here,
   and every later section inherits it.
2. **Section F** — sourcing. An unsourced claim under the brand's name is a defect of the same
   class as an unsourced figure in a decision document.
3. **D.1** — distribution at commission. The most common reason good work has no effect.
4. **E.3** — proxy substitution in the success definition. It hides the fact that nothing is
   being measured.
5. **C.4 / C.5** — shelf life and its maintenance obligation. Unowned evergreen becomes the
   archive's liability.
6. **B / G / H** — plan fit, archive obligations, boundary.
