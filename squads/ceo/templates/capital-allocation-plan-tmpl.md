**If debt is used:** coverage under the base case *and* under the stated downside case; maturity
wall; what happens if credit conditions are poor at the refinancing moment; which doors close if
conditions turn — typically reinvestment and opportunistic repurchase, at exactly the moment
both become cheapest.

**If equity is issued:** dilution stated in per-share terms, and the issue price compared to a
defensible intrinsic-value estimate. Equity issued below intrinsic value transfers value from
existing holders and rarely appears as a cost anywhere in a plan.

## 9. Guards applied

- [ ] **Sunk cost guard** — money already spent on this initiative is not an input to whether the next unit should go there. Confirm nothing in the recommendation rests on it.
- [ ] **Peer guard** — any "everyone is consolidating / everyone is buying back" argument is recorded **here, separately**, so it cannot substitute for a return case: [record it or write NONE]
- [ ] **Management attention** — priced as a cost in the same units as everything else, because it is the scarcest input and is almost never priced: [estimate]
- [ ] **Synergy separation** (acquisitions) — cost synergies and revenue synergies stated separately, with the result also shown **without** revenue synergies
- [ ] **Dilution check** (repurchase) — is this reducing share count, or offsetting issuance from equity compensation? Different decisions, frequently conflated
- [ ] **Timing discipline** (repurchase) — opportunistic and price-driven, or programmatic and calendar-driven? A calendar-driven programme is not an investment decision and is labelled a distribution policy
- [ ] **Intrinsic value estimate** (repurchase) — stated with its inputs and its uncertainty range. Without it there is no test, only a share-count reduction
