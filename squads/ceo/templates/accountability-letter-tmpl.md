[[LLM: TEMPLATE INSTRUCTIONS — ACCOUNTABILITY LETTER

The ordering of the sections is the method. Do not reorder it.

1. THE ACCOUNT COMES FIRST. A letter that opens with new plans and defers the account
   trains the reader to expect exactly that, and the deferral is noticed long before the
   content is.
2. FACTS BEFORE INTERPRETATION. A paragraph that tells the reader what to think before it
   tells them what happened reads as an argument, and is discounted as one.
3. MISSES GET THE SAME PROMINENCE AS WINS — same section, same voice, same type size.
   Never a subordinate clause, never an appendix, never a sentence that begins with a
   success.
4. REPORT IN THE ORIGINAL TERMS. If a metric was redefined, report BOTH and say it was
   redefined, when, and by whom. Never report only the new one.
5. USE DIFFERENT WORDS FOR KNOWN, ESTIMATED AND HOPED. A document expressing all three in
   the same register is misleading even when every individual sentence is defensible.

Every figure carries a source and a date. Anything unsourced is marked UNVERIFIED and does
not enter a document that leaves the company (Constitution Article IV).]]

## 7. What we expect next

> Every forward statement carries a commitment level, in language distinct enough that the
> level survives being quoted out of context.

| Statement | Level | Metric / observable | Due date | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| | COMMITTED / FORECAST / ASPIRATION | | | |

**Level definitions, restated in the document itself so the reader shares them:**

- **COMMITTED** — we will do this; hold us to it. *Must be within our control, measurable, and dated. If any of the three fails, it is not a commitment and is relabelled.*
- **FORECAST** — our best estimate, **with a range** and the assumptions it rests on. *A forecast without a range is read as a commitment.*
- **ASPIRATION** — what we are aiming at. Not a commitment.

**Commitment count:** [n]. A long list of commitments is a long list of future misses. State why
this set is the smallest that conveys the direction.

**Registered:** every item above has been written to the promise register before this letter is
released. A forward statement that is not in the register cannot be accounted for later, which
is how records become unreadable.
