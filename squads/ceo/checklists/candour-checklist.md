# Candour Checklist

**Checklist ID:** CEO-CL-004
**Referenced by:** stakeholder-lead (Herald)
**Method source:** No single canonical work, and none is claimed. This checklist encodes the
observable requirements of a documented professional genre — annual shareholder letters, board
reporting packs and management reports: state the commitment before the period, report against it
afterwards in the same terms, give misses the same prominence as successes, separate what is
known from what is estimated and from what is hoped, explain variance with a mechanism rather
than a mood, and keep the record so a reader can compare what was said with what happened. In
some jurisdictions parts of this are codified for public companies — United States securities
filings, for example, require a narrative discussion section alongside the financial statements.
That is named as evidence that the genre has formal conventions, **not** as a standard this
squad applies or interprets. Every procedure below is this squad's own.

**Purpose:** Scan a draft — board packet, investor update, annual letter, internal brief,
reversal notice — before any human sees it as a candidate for release.

> **Nothing on this checklist is a compliance determination.** Section 8 routes disclosure,
> securities, accounting and employment questions to qualified human counsel. This squad
> identifies the flags; counsel decides.

[[LLM: INITIALIZATION — CANDOUR SCAN

Output format for every finding: **the quote**, its **classification**, and the **plain

## 1. The account exists and comes first — BLOCKING

- [ ] Every promise from the register that comes due in this period appears in the draft
- [ ] Any promise due and **not** accounted for is named explicitly with the reason, rather than silently absent
- [ ] The account appears **before** new plans. A document that opens with new plans and defers the account trains the reader to expect that deferral
- [ ] Each account entry follows the fixed order: original wording → what happened → the variance → the cause → what changes
- [ ] The **original wording** is quoted, not paraphrased
- [ ] Each promise carries the commitment level **as it was expressed at the time**, including AMBIGUOUS where the level was never stated
- [ ] Every entry states **what changes as a result**. An account with no consequence teaches the reader that the promises do not matter

**FAIL → the draft is a plan, not an account.** Return it.

---

## 2. Misses have the same prominence as wins — BLOCKING

- [ ] Misses appear in the same section as achievements, not grouped separately or moved to an appendix
- [ ] No miss appears in a subordinate clause
- [ ] No miss appears in a sentence that begins with a success
- [ ] Misses are written in the same voice and at the same length as comparable achievements
- [ ] A "what we got wrong" section exists and is findable without searching
- [ ] Lessons recorded there are specific. A generic lesson — one that could have been written before the period — is worse than none and is removed
- [ ] The record is not a selection. If this period contains only successes, the draft states how many promises came due and how many were met, so the reader can see it is a record rather than a highlight reel

**FAIL → the record is a selection, and readers discount everything in it accordingly.**

---

## 3. Reporting in the original terms

- [ ] Every metric is reported in the **terms and units it was promised in**
- [ ] Where a metric was redefined, **both** are reported, with the fact of redefinition, when, and by whom
- [ ] No metric is reported **only** in its new definition
- [ ] Units are consistent with previous periods, or the change is stated
- [ ] The variance is stated numerically where possible, **without qualifiers** attached to the number
- [ ] Placed beside the previous period's document, nothing appears to have been quietly dropped
- [ ] Nothing the audience already knows is left unmentioned — an unmentioned known fact is read as concealment

---

## 4. Language scan — quote every instance

### 4a. Hedged verbs
- [ ] No "we aim to", "we are working towards", "we continue to focus on", "we remain committed to" where a **commitment** or a **miss** is the actual subject

*Replacement pattern: state the commitment with its level and date, or state the miss with its gap.*

### 4b. Passive constructions hiding an actor
- [ ] No "the target was not met", "delays were experienced", "challenges were encountered", "the decision was taken"

*Replacement pattern: name who and what.*

### 4c. Restated targets
- [ ] No metric reported in different terms than it was promised (cross-check against Section 3)

### 4d. Buried misses
- [ ] No miss in a subordinate clause, an appendix, or a sentence opening with a success (cross-check against Section 2)

### 4e. Interpretation before fact
- [ ] No paragraph tells the reader what to think before it tells them what happened
- [ ] Section 1 of the letter contains observable results only, with no interpretation

### 4f. Uniform register
- [ ] Known, estimated and hoped are expressed in **distinguishably different language**
- [ ] Every figure that is an estimate is marked as an estimate
- [ ] Every estimate carries its confidence or range — a figure presented without its uncertainty will be quoted back without it

### 4g. Externalised cause
- [ ] Not every variance is attributed to conditions outside the company
- [ ] What was controlled and what was not are both stated, and the second is not overweighted
- [ ] The cause is a **mechanism**, not a mood. "Demand softened" fails; "two of four concentrated renewals slipped a quarter, which is [x]% of recurring revenue" passes

### 4h. Euphemised reversal
- [ ] The words **refinement, evolution, clarification, pivot** do not describe a change of direction anywhere in the draft
- [ ] Every reversal states the reversal in its first sentence: "We said X. We are now doing the opposite of X."
- [ ] The earlier statement is quoted with its date, so the reader does not have to find it
- [ ] "We were wrong then" and "the world changed" are distinguished honestly — readers can usually tell which it was, and resent the wrong one being claimed
- [ ] What the reversal costs is stated: sunk effort, affected commitments, people affected

---

## 5. Forward statements

- [ ] Every forward statement carries a level: **COMMITTED / FORECAST / ASPIRATION**
- [ ] The levels are expressed in language distinct enough to survive being quoted out of context
- [ ] Each COMMITTED item is within our control, measurable and dated. If any of the three fails, it is relabelled rather than kept
- [ ] Each FORECAST states a **range** and the assumptions it rests on. A forecast without a range is read as a commitment
- [ ] The number of committed items is counted, and is the smallest set that conveys the direction — a long list of commitments is a long list of future misses
- [ ] Every forward statement in this draft has been written to the **promise register before release**. A forward statement not in the register cannot be accounted for later
- [ ] No forward statement is more confident than the decision record behind it

---

## 6. Narrative honesty

- [ ] **No narrative the underlying decision does not support.** If the analysis was thin, the draft says the decision was made with thin evidence
- [ ] Improving a decision through prose has not been attempted anywhere
- [ ] Where a specialist recorded dissent, it appears **verbatim**, not summarised into a caveat
- [ ] Alternatives rejected are stated fairly, in their strongest form
- [ ] What was given up is stated. Where nothing was given up, the draft says the decision was an announcement
- [ ] Every claim traces to a decision record, a specialist artifact, or a system of record
- [ ] No figure appears without a source and a date
- [ ] Unsourced material is marked UNVERIFIED and removed from anything leaving the company (Constitution Article IV)

---

## 7. Audience and sequence

- [ ] An audience map exists for this message
- [ ] Depth differs by audience where appropriate; **facts do not differ anywhere**
- [ ] **Consistency test:** if all audiences compared what they received, none would conclude something incompatible with another. If any would, the set is **rebuilt, not resequenced**
- [ ] For each audience, what they must **not** wrongly conclude from this message is named
- [ ] The sequence orders audiences by the cost of learning it second-hand — those most affected and least able to absorb it publicly go first
- [ ] The interval between first and last audience is set: long enough for individual conversations to happen properly, short enough not to open a leak window
- [ ] A deliverer is named for each step — cascades fail at the step where nobody is named
- [ ] Nobody directly affected learns this from a broadcast rather than from their own manager
- [ ] An employee reading both the internal brief and the external document would find them consistent
- [ ] Where a decision affects individuals' roles or employment, those individual conversations belong to qualified human managers with HR counsel and happen **first**

---

## 8. Counsel flags — identified, never resolved — BLOCKING

Flag every instance. State the kind of review each needs. Resolve none.

- [ ] Any forward-looking statement to investors or prospective investors
- [ ] Any figure that could be characterised as financial guidance
- [ ] Any statement about a transaction, a raise, a distribution or a repurchase
- [ ] Any statement about individuals' employment, departures or restructuring
- [ ] Any statement about a customer, partner or counterparty **by name**
- [ ] Any statement touching a contractual obligation, covenant or regulatory filing
- [ ] Any comparison to a competitor
- [ ] Any accounting figure that has not come from the system of record
- [ ] Any statement about a security incident, an outage or a data matter
- [ ] Each flag names a **kind of review** and a **named human reviewer**
- [ ] No item in the draft opines on disclosure obligations, securities law, tax or accounting treatment

**FAIL → the draft does not go to any external audience.** Flags are cleared by named humans first.

---

## 9. Adversarial read

- [ ] **Hostile but fair reader:** the ten questions such a reader asks after this draft are listed. The draft answers at least seven. If fewer, it is incomplete
- [ ] **Quotable sentence:** the sentence most likely to be quoted back in twelve months is identified, and it is one we are content to be held to
- [ ] **Omission test:** what the audience already knows and this document does not mention is listed, and each omission is deliberate and defensible
- [ ] **Comparison test:** placed beside the previous period's document, nothing appears quietly dropped
- [ ] **Internal-external test:** the internal brief and the external document are consistent
- [ ] Findings are reported to the author. The draft is **not silently rewritten**

---

## 10. Voice and authorship

- [ ] The document is written in the **company's own voice**
- [ ] It does not adopt the voice, cadence or signature phrasing of any known author of shareholder letters. Imitation is detectable and discredits the content
- [ ] Nothing is quoted from, or attributed to, any named person who did not say it
- [ ] No source is invented to lend authority to a procedure. Where a procedure is this squad's own, it is labelled as this squad's
- [ ] **No document is sent by this agent.** This is a draft for human review, and the human who sends it owns it

---

## 11. Boundary

- [ ] The draft **reports** decisions; it does not make them
- [ ] Where the request was to justify a decision that has not been analysed, the work was returned to `@ceo:strategy-lead`, `@ceo:capital-allocator` or `@ceo:org-designer` rather than written around
- [ ] No strategy diagnosis, capital arithmetic or org design is produced here
- [ ] No epic, PRD or story appears (that is `@pm`, `@sm`)

---

## Verdict

| Outcome | Condition | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| **NOT AN ACCOUNT** | Section 1 fails | Return. The draft is a plan wearing a report's structure |
| **SELECTION, NOT A RECORD** | Section 2 fails | Return. Restore the misses to the same prominence |
| **BLOCKED FROM EXTERNAL RELEASE** | Section 8 fails | Route flags to named human counsel before anything leaves |
| **FINDINGS RECORDED** | Blocking sections pass; residual findings elsewhere | Return the quote / classification / replacement set to the author. Author decides; findings accepted rather than repaired are recorded in the release record |
| **CLEAN** | No findings | Proceed to human review and a named human sender |

**A finding may be accepted. It may not be removed from the record of what was found.**
