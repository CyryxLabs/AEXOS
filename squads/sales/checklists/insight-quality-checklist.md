# Insight Quality Checklist

**Checklist ID:** SALES-CL-004
**Referenced by:** method-lead (`*build-insight`, `*build-reframe`, `*insight-audit`, `*tension-check`)
**Purpose:** Test a commercial insight, a reframe, or an existing pitch before it is used with a buyer. Any **STOP** item that fails means the insight is rebuilt or abandoned — not softened, not caveated, not shipped with a disclaimer.

## 1. STOP — Is there an insight at all?

- [ ] The buyer learns something about **their own business** that they did not know before the conversation
- [ ] It can be stated in **one sentence**
- [ ] It is not a capability description wearing the word "insight"
- [ ] It is not category education — an explanation of why this class of product exists
- [ ] It does not merely confirm what the buyer already believes

**Classification of the material reviewed:** capability description / category education / **commercial insight**

> If the buyer learns nothing, the meeting is a status update. Buyers do not allocate budget after status updates.

## 2. STOP — Is it provable outside our own marketing?

- [ ] Every claim has a named source
- [ ] At least one source is **not** our own marketing: our aggregated account data with a showable distribution, published industry or regulatory data, or the buyer's own numbers
- [ ] The buyer could verify it, fully or partially, if they chose to
- [ ] Claims sourced only to our materials are marked UNVERIFIED **and removed from the pitch**

> A confident, unverifiable assertion designed to change a buyer's spending decision is manipulation regardless of intent. It is also weak: it survives exactly until a competent buyer asks where the number came from, which in a serious evaluation is always.

## 3. STOP — Does it lead to our unique strengths?

- [ ] Ask directly: **could a competitor deliver this identical insight, word for word, and benefit equally?** If yes, it fails
- [ ] The unique attribute the insight leads to is **named**
- [ ] That attribute is traceable to the positioning artifact from `@products:positioning-lead`
- [ ] If no such artifact exists, or the attribute is not on it, that is recorded as a **positioning defect and routed outward** — not improvised inside a call plan

> A rule-1 failure is the most expensive kind: a true, well-delivered, category-generic insight educates the buyer to run a market evaluation at our expense.

## 4. STOP — Does it challenge an assumption they actually hold?

- [ ] The belief being challenged is stated **in the buyer's language**
- [ ] It is a belief real buyers in this segment hold, not a strawman constructed to be knocked down
- [ ] We can name where we learned they hold it

## 5. The reframe has four lines

- [ ] Line 1 — what they believe
- [ ] Line 2 — what is actually true, with evidence attached
- [ ] Line 3 — what the gap costs, in **their** units, with the figure labelled ours or theirs
- [ ] Line 4 — **why it was invisible from where they sit** *(mandatory)*

**On line 4:** without it, a correct reframe reads as an accusation of carelessness. The buyer stops evaluating the problem and starts defending their judgement. With it, the same content reads as "here is something you could not have seen from inside one company."

- [ ] Line 4 explains a **structural** reason the problem was hard to see — data not aggregated, report format hides the split, pattern only visible across many companies
- [ ] Line 4 does not imply the buyer should have noticed

## 6. Catalyzes action, and scales

- [ ] It changes what the buyer would do **next week**, not only what they think
- [ ] Where possible, it redirects a decision already in motion (a budget request, an approved project)
- [ ] It applies across the segment, not to exactly one account
- [ ] The **segment boundary is stated** — where the insight is false, and what we do for accounts outside it

## 7. The choreography

- [ ] The six steps are written as a **runnable outline**, not described
- [ ] Step 1 (Warmer) names problems before asking questions, and expects a correction
- [ ] Step 2 (Reframe) is delivered as an observation with evidence, not as a challenge
- [ ] Step 3 (Rational Drowning) quantifies in the buyer's units and asks them to supply or correct a number
- [ ] Step 4 (Emotional Impact) uses their described situation, with a date — never a manufactured scenario
- [ ] Step 5 (A New Way) lists required capabilities with **no vendor named** *(if we are named here, the sequence has collapsed into a pitch — this is a STOP)*
- [ ] Step 6 (Our Solution) is the first mention of us, and includes the explicit **not-list**
- [ ] The money conversation is placed early and explicitly, with order of magnitude and what moves it
- [ ] Each step earns the next

## 8. Tailoring

- [ ] The **reframe is constant** across stakeholders; only the concrete consequence varies
- [ ] Each stakeholder row records what they are measured on, what the change costs them personally, and what they risk if it fails
- [ ] Every stakeholder has **one sentence they would repeat to a colleague**. If it cannot be written, the tailoring is not done
- [ ] Internal product vocabulary is stripped; the language is theirs
- [ ] Whoever **loses** from this change is named, with their likely objection, and is engaged rather than routed around

## 9. Tension — constructive or coercive

Every planned move is screened. Any failure **blocks** the move.

- [ ] The pressure is a real constraint on our side or theirs, not created for effect
- [ ] Every stated consequence can be substantiated if challenged
- [ ] No move implies scarcity, deadline or competitive risk that does not exist
- [ ] No peer shame ("most of your peers have already solved this"), and no undefined "you are behind"
- [ ] No capability claimed that does not exist today; roadmap is disclosed as roadmap
- [ ] Known limitations, integration gaps and total cost appear **inside** the teaching conversation
- [ ] **Reversal test:** if the buyer saw exactly how each move was constructed, would they still consider the conversation fair?
- [ ] Every blocked move has a written compliant alternative that states our real constraint and asks for theirs

> Tension is disagreement with the status quo, created by evidence, in service of a goal the buyer has already stated. Pressure is discomfort created for our timeline. They are not the same move.

## 10. Consensus materials, if the advocate is being equipped

- [ ] The advocate has passed the champion willingness test, confirmed with `@sales:qualification-lead`, before any of this is built
- [ ] A one-paragraph problem statement exists that they can circulate **with our name off it**
- [ ] An objection map exists: each stakeholder's likely concern, in their words, with a two-line answer the advocate can give unaided
- [ ] The explicit not-list is included — it is the item that most raises their internal credibility
- [ ] **Nothing in the kit is unverified.** Our modelled figures and the buyer's own figures are clearly separated, so a challenged derivation does not cost the advocate their standing *(STOP)*

## 11. Honesty of direction

- [ ] If the honest reframe points **away** from our solution for this buyer, that is stated and routed to `@sales:qualification-lead` for disqualification
- [ ] A reframe is a claim about the buyer's business. It does not become false because it is commercially inconvenient

## 12. Boundary

- [ ] Nothing here sets or implies a price, discount or package (`@products:pricing-strategist`)
- [ ] Nothing here defines a market category or a competitive frame of reference (`@products:positioning-lead`)
- [ ] No qualification verdict is issued (`@sales:qualification-lead`)
- [ ] No concession or term is designed (`@sales:negotiation-lead`)
- [ ] Team-wide method gaps are routed to `@sales:pipeline-ops` as training-formula findings, not recorded as individual failures
