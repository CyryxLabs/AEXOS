# Commercial Integrity Screen Checklist

**Checklist ID:** SALES-CL-001
**Referenced by:** sales-chief (`*ethics-check`), negotiation-lead (`*integrity-screen`), qualification-lead
**Purpose:** Screen a proposed commercial move before it is made. Any failed item **blocks the move**. A blocked move is replaced with the compliant alternative, never softened, never rephrased into something that passes on wording alone.

[[LLM: EXECUTION INSTRUCTIONS — COMMERCIAL INTEGRITY SCREEN

1. Run this BEFORE the move is used, not after. A screen applied to a move already made is a post-mortem.
2. Mark each item PASS, **BLOCK**, or N/A. There is no "partial".
3. A single BLOCK stops the move. Do not proceed on a majority of passes.
4. For every BLOCK, write the compliant alternative in Section 8 using the construction rule:
   state our real constraint honestly, then ask for theirs. If no legitimate goal survives the
   rewrite, say plainly that the legitimate goal does not exist.
5. Record the verdict against the deal record. If a human proceeds against the screen, log it as
   decided against advice, with the name of the decider.
6. This screen never argues that a move is "effective". Effectiveness is not one of the tests.]]

## 1. Urgency — is it real and correctly attributed?

- [ ] Every deadline stated is a real date with a real consequence, not one created for effect
- [ ] The deadline is attributed to whoever actually owns it — our approval cycle is stated as **ours**, never presented as the buyer's problem or as a market rule
- [ ] No expiry, price reversion or "this offer ends" is claimed unless it is an actual documented rule we would apply to any account
- [ ] Our own quarter boundary is never dressed as a constraint on the buyer

> **BLOCK example:** "Q3 pricing expires Friday." Friday is our forecast cut-off.
> **Compliant form:** "Our approvals for this structure run on a quarterly cycle, so a decision before the end of the quarter keeps this exact structure available without re-approval. That is my constraint, not a deadline for you. What would have to happen on your side for a decision to be possible by then?"

## 2. Scarcity — is it real and verifiable?

- [ ] No implementation slot, capacity limit, cohort or allocation is claimed that does not exist and is not tracked somewhere checkable
- [ ] No "another client is waiting" unless a specific, real, named commitment exists
- [ ] Any genuine constraint stated could be evidenced if the buyer asked to see it

## 3. Alternatives — is every implied comparison true?

- [ ] No competing offer, other buyer or alternative bidder is implied unless it exists
- [ ] No claim about what competitors do, charge or cannot do that we cannot source outside our own marketing
- [ ] Our real alternatives (pipeline coverage, whether we need this deal this quarter) are the only leverage represented

> A bluffed alternative is the cheapest claim in a negotiation to test: the counterparty simply waits. When it fails to materialize, every other statement we have made is discounted.

## 4. Walk-away — would we actually take it?

- [ ] No walk-away is announced that we would not take
- [ ] The walk-away was written before the conversation, not discovered during it
- [ ] The person with authority to change the walk-away is named, and it is not the rep under pressure

## 5. Capability — is every claim true today?

- [ ] Every capability described exists today and is in the shipped product
- [ ] Roadmap is disclosed **as roadmap**, with no implied date we have not committed to internally
- [ ] No reference, logo, case study, metric or "most of your peers" claim is used unless it is real and attributable
- [ ] No number is quoted whose source we cannot name

## 6. Material omission — is anything being withheld?

- [ ] Known functional limitations relevant to the buyer's stated criteria are disclosed
- [ ] Known integration gaps are disclosed
- [ ] Total cost is disclosed: implementation, onboarding, professional services, required third-party licences
- [ ] Renewal mechanics are disclosed, including any uplift, auto-renewal window or repricing trigger
- [ ] Any decision criterion we cannot satisfy is stated plainly rather than managed around

> Concealing an unmet criterion is not a sales tactic. It converts a lost deal into a churned customer, and it is a disqualification signal — route to `@sales:qualification-lead`.

## 7. Pressure, empathy and the person

- [ ] No move uses an understanding of the counterparty's fear to press on it rather than to serve their interest (**empathy inversion**)
- [ ] No move implies personal or professional consequence for the individual if they do not proceed
- [ ] No move asks a champion or internal advocate to assert something we have not verified — their credibility is not a resource we get to spend
- [ ] No move attacks the buyer's competence; discomfort, where it exists, comes from evidence about their operation

## 8. The two whole-move tests

- [ ] **Reversal test.** If the buyer learned exactly how this move was constructed — the reasoning, the framing, what we knew — would they still consider the dealing fair?
- [ ] **Durability test.** Could the buyer defend the resulting agreement to their own organization if they knew everything we know? An agreement they could not defend is a renegotiation scheduled for renewal, usually with someone new in the seat.

**Construction rule for every compliant alternative:** state our real constraint accurately, attribute it to us, and end with a question that hands the buyer the problem to solve *with* us — not a claim that pressures them. If their answer is that nothing can happen in the timeframe, that is information the forecast needs and it is worth more than the pressure would have been.
