| Strategy artifact used (path + date) | |

| # | Criterion (capability + evidence, never an attribute) | MUST / STRONGLY PREFERRED / NICE |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Has led an organization through a transition from X to Y, evidenced by Z" | |

Criteria are written as **capabilities and evidence**. "Visionary" is not a criterion. A list where
everything is essential has not been prioritized and will be applied inconsistently.

### Incumbent-contamination test — both directions

| Direction | Question | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Forward | Would this criterion be here if the current chief executive were someone else? | |
| **Inverse** | Does this list **omit** anything the incumbent is weak at that the next phase requires? | |

The inverse direction is nearly invisible and usually more consequential. Run it explicitly and
record the answer.

## 3. Bench — readiness horizons with evidence

**Precondition:** agreed criteria exist. A bench review without criteria produces a popularity
ranking.

| Candidate | Criteria met (with observed evidence) | Criteria not evidenced | Horizon | Development action that closes the gap | Owner | Years on bench |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Horizons** — "talented" is not a horizon and is not accepted as an answer:

| Horizon | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `READY NOW` | Meets the criteria against observed evidence; could take the role this quarter |
| `READY 1–2` | Meets most criteria; the gap is a named experience or level transition with development underway |
| `READY 3+` | Strong at the current level; no evidence yet at the next, and the transition has not begun |
| `NOT ON THIS PATH` | Valuable in the current role; these criteria are not the direction of their development |
| `UNVERIFIED` | Regarded highly, with no observed evidence against criteria. **This is not a rating** |

**Evidence types**, marked per criterion: `DELIVERED` (they did this, at this scale, with this
result) / `OBSERVED` (behaviour seen directly by a board member) / `REPORTED` (someone else's
account) / `INFERRED` (no direct evidence — marked as a **gap**, not a rating).

### Mandatory findings

- [ ] **Bench-of-one rule.** Where exactly **one** internal candidate is named for a role, this plan
      states that **there is no internal succession for that role, plus one person who might work
      out.** Applied without exception, because the alternative reads as coverage.
      *(`CONSTRUCTION`.)*
- [ ] **Observation gap.** Where the board has **never directly observed** a candidate, that is
      stated as a finding. It is the most common and most correctable weakness in internal
      succession, and it is closed by exposure to the board over time — not by a longer interview.
      *(`CONSTRUCTION`.)*
- [ ] **Stalled bench.** Anyone on the bench three or more years without moving is named. That is a
      development failure, a retention risk, or a rating that was never honest.
- [ ] **Pipeline check.** What change in **skills, time application and values** does the next level
      require, and is the candidate showing any of it yet? The most common failure is excellence at
      the previous level.
      *Attribution: level-transition reasoning is from Charan, Drotter & Noel, ***The Leadership
      Pipeline*** (2001) — a **different book with different co-authors** from* Boards That Lead*.
      The two are never merged or cited for each other's content.*

### Interim mandate — the boundary, written explicitly

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| What the interim **may** decide | |
| What the interim **may not** decide | |
| **Is the interim a candidate for the permanent role?** | YES / NO — state it. Ambiguity distorts everyone's behaviour immediately |

## 6. Internal versus external — structured, not a preference

| Variable | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Continuity value** — how much of what makes this work is relationship, context and tacit knowledge that leaves with a departure? | |
| **Change mandate** — how much must be different in the next phase, and can someone who built the current state credibly dismantle parts of it? | |
| **Bench reality** — is there an internal candidate **ready now against the criteria, honestly rated**, or is the internal option really a development plan? | |
| **Integration risk** — culture strength, role dependence on internal relationships, and the historical record of external senior hires here | |

**Asymmetry, stated explicitly:** internal appointments fail slowly and visibly; external
appointments fail fast and expensively. Neither pattern is an argument on its own, and both are
usually the whole of the unstated argument in the room.

**Honest default:** where the change mandate is high and the bench is not ready against criteria,
**external is the indicated answer**, and the correct response to disliking that is to fix the bench
years earlier — not to relax the criteria now.

## 7. Executive assessment — three strands, never netted

| Strand | Assessment | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|
| **Results** — what was delivered | | DELIVERED / REPORTED |
| **Capability for the next phase** — against the strategy-derived criteria | | OBSERVED / DELIVERED / INFERRED (gap) |
| **Conduct** | | OBSERVED / REPORTED |

**Binding rule:** the three are assessed and reported **separately**, never combined into a single
verdict. A strong result strand does not offset a conduct finding, and netting them is how conduct
findings disappear. *(`CONSTRUCTION`.)*

**Out of scope, explicitly:** cultural-fit judgements not written into the criteria; anything
requiring employment-law input; any characterization of the person beyond the criteria.

**Figures used in this assessment:** whether they have been challenged is `audit-lead`'s question,
not this plan's. State which figures were relied on and whether they carry assurance.
