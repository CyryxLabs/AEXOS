## 3. The operated test — applied to every PRESENT mark

**The distinction this audit exists to enforce: the provision exists, versus the provision
operated.** Ordinary governance reporting collapses these into one status and thereby reports a
filing cabinet as a governance system.

| Provision | Artifact exists? | Evidence it was USED in the period | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| | Yes / No | Minutes, decisions tested against it, advice actually taken, a fee actually set | PRESENT / downgraded to ASSERTED |

Worked instances of the downgrade:

- A committee with written terms of reference and **no minutes** in the period → `ASSERTED`
- A schedule of reserved matters against which **no decision was ever tested** → `ASSERTED`
- A procedure for independent professional advice that **no member has ever used, and which no
  member can describe** → `ASSERTED`
- A register of interests **last updated before two of the current members joined** → `ASSERTED`
- A separation of chair and chief executive where the **executive sets the agenda and controls the
  information flow** → `ASSERTED`; nominal separation is not separation

Every downgrade names the evidence that would restore the `PRESENT` mark and who holds it.

## 4. Gap ranking — by structural consequence, never by effort to fix

| Rank | Class | Why it ranks here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gaps that permit **unfettered decision by one individual** | Nothing else in the structure functions if this one does |
| 2 | Gaps **in the record** — decisions that cannot be shown to have been taken properly | The decision may have been sound; it cannot be demonstrated to have been |
| 3 | **Disclosure** gaps | Material, but downstream of both of the above |

| Rank | Gap | Provision at stake | Source mark |
|---|---|---|---|

A gap is never promoted because it is easy to close, nor demoted because it is hard. The ranking is
of consequence; the sequencing of work is a separate decision the board takes with this ranking in
front of it.

## 6. Attribution pass — run over the finished document

Every recommendation is marked exactly one of:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CADBURY` | Traceable to the Cadbury Report (1992) or its Code of Best Practice — the provision is named |
| `DERIVED` | Successor codes or established practice; the instrument is named for orientation only, and its provisions are **not** paraphrased as though verified |
| `CONSTRUCTION` | This agent's own operational test |
| `UNSOURCED` | Could not be traced — reported as unsourced rather than attributed to something plausible |

**Binding rules.**

1. **Never present a general governance practice as a Code provision.**
2. **Never invent a citation, title, year or provision.** Where the exact text of a provision is
   uncertain, describe the principle and instruct the reader to consult the source. A wrong
   attribution is worse than no attribution, because it is relied upon exactly as if it were
   verified.
3. **Successor instruments** (Greenbury 1995, Hampel 1998, the Combined Code 1998, Turnbull 1999,
   Higgs 2003, Smith 2003, the UK Corporate Governance Code from 2010, the OECD Principles) are
   named for **orientation only**. Their versions, numbering and content change over time. Do not
   cite any of them from memory; cite the source document and its edition, or do not cite it.
