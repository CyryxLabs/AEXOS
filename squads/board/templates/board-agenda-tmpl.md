## Construction rules — applied before the table is filled

1. **Every item carries a type.** DECISION (the board must resolve), OVERSIGHT (the board must
   challenge and then accept or reject the evidence), INFORMATION (no decision is sought — labelled
   so, so that an update cannot later be cited as an approval), STANDING.
2. **Every DECISION item names its required evidence.** If the evidence does not exist, the item is
   not ready: it moves to the next meeting with an owner and a date. It is not discussed anyway and
   half-decided.
3. **Time is allocated in inverse proportion to management's comfort.** The item that was asked to
   be "run through quickly" gets the protected slot.
4. **Decision items precede presentations.** A presentation never precedes the decision it is meant
   to support.
5. **Executive session is a standing item**, scheduled every meeting, so that convening one is
   never itself a signal.
6. **Every item ends with a disposition.** Approved, noted, deferred with a date, or rejected with
   a reason. The disposition column is left open at issue and completed in the minute.
7. **An item deferred twice becomes a standing item** with a named owner until closed.

## Construction notes — written, not implied

State for the record:

- **Why the longest slot went where it did.**
- **Which items are explicitly INFORMATION**, and the reason that label matters for this meeting.
- **Which item could plausibly end in a "no".** If none can, the board is ratifying rather than
  deciding, and that is itself an agenda finding.
- **Which question management would prefer not be asked**, and where on the agenda it sits.

## Attribution

Agenda discipline as applied here is **this squad's own construction** — an orchestration practice,
not a provision of any published code. Two elements do trace to a named source and are marked where
used:

- The board should **meet regularly, retain full and effective control, and monitor the executive
  management**, and should have a **formal schedule of matters specifically reserved to it for
  decision** — *Report of the Committee on the Financial Aspects of Corporate Governance* (the
  **Cadbury Report**, United Kingdom, December 1992), Code of Best Practice.
- The **private session with the auditors without management present** is common professional
  practice in audit committee work, which has **no single canonical author**; it is attributed to
  no one and marked `DISCIPLINE`.

Nothing else on this page is a code provision, and it is not presented as one. Where a specific
provision matters to a decision, read it in the source document rather than relying on this
summary.
