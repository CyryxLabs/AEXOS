# Governance Audit Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BRD-CL-002
**Referenced by:** governance-counsel
**Applied to:** any governance audit, reserved-matters schedule, delegation map, committee charter,
independence test, conflict register or comply-or-explain statement before it leaves the agent
**Purpose:** Enforce the two tests the method turns on — **exists versus operated**, and
**attributed versus asserted** — and stop any output that reads as a legal or compliance
conclusion.

[[LLM: INITIALIZATION — GOVERNANCE AUDIT QUALITY BAR

Run this against the finished artifact.

Mark each item:
  [x] satisfied — name the passage in the artifact that satisfies it
  [ ] not satisfied — name what is missing
  [N/A] not applicable — state why

BLOCKING items stop the artifact from being issued. There is no partial credit on a blocking item:
an audit issued with a blocking gap is read as though the gap were not there, which is precisely
the failure this checklist prevents.]]

## 1. Scope and adaptation

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The organization is named and the body being treated as "the board" is named unambiguously
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The adaptation statement appears in the **body** of the artifact, not in a footnote, and names the specific differences between this organization and the Cadbury Code's original scope (the boards of UK-registered listed companies)
- [ ] The period used for the operated test is stated
- [ ] Every artifact examined is listed by path or source
- [ ] Every artifact **sought and not found** is listed as an evidence gap — not silently skipped
- [ ] Unresolvable supplied paths are reported as evidence gaps rather than dropped

## 2. The four marks

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Every provision carries exactly one of `PRESENT`, `ASSERTED`, `ABSENT`, `NOT APPLICABLE`. No provision is left unmarked
- [ ] Each `NOT APPLICABLE` states its reason, and the reason is about the organization's structure — not about the difficulty of assessing it
- [ ] No status word outside the four is used. "In place", "broadly compliant", "largely satisfied" do not appear
- [ ] `ABSENT` and `ASSERTED` are distinguished throughout — a claimed-but-unevidenced provision is not reported as missing, and a missing one is not reported as claimed

## 3. The operated test — the distinction the method exists to enforce

**A provision that exists is not a provision that operated.** Collapsing the two is how a filing
cabinet gets reported as a governance system.

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The operated test was applied to **every** `PRESENT` mark, and the evidence of operation within the period is named for each
- [ ] Any `PRESENT` mark that failed the operated test was **downgraded to `ASSERTED`** — not annotated and left as PRESENT
- [ ] A committee with terms of reference and no minutes in the period is `ASSERTED`
- [ ] A reserved-matters schedule against which no decision was tested is `ASSERTED`
- [ ] An independent-advice procedure no member has used, and which no member can describe, is `ASSERTED`
- [ ] A register of interests not updated since the current composition changed is `ASSERTED`
- [ ] A nominal separation of chair and chief executive, where the executive sets the agenda and controls the information flow to the board, is `ASSERTED` — nominal separation is not separation
- [ ] Each downgrade names the evidence that would restore `PRESENT`, and who holds it

## 4. Structural tests actually applied

- [ ] The **removal-power test** was applied to every independence claim: can the person being supervised initiate or effectively determine this member's removal, reappointment or fee? A yes fails independence **structurally**, regardless of every other answer. This test is marked `CONSTRUCTION` where used
- [ ] The **quorum test** was applied at board level: are the independent members of sufficient calibre and number for their views to carry significant weight? One independent voice against a unified executive is not a quorum. This test is attributed to the Cadbury Code
- [ ] Where the chair and chief executive roles are **combined**, the artifact does **not** default to recommending separation. It specifies the compensating strong and independent element the Code requires, including a recognised senior member, and states concretely what that member must be able to do
- [ ] The compensating element was itself tested for reality: who appoints the senior independent member, who can remove them, and who sets their remuneration
- [ ] Delegation authority and the **reporting obligation back to the board were tested separately**. A delegation with a limit but no report back is unmonitored
- [ ] Value thresholds were checked for **decay**: is there an indexation or annual-review rule? A nominal threshold never revisited delegates progressively more with no visible moment of failure
- [ ] Conflicts: the artifact states that **disclosure alone is not a remedy** — every material conflict carries a recorded participation decision (present and voting / present and abstaining / absent from the discussion)

## 5. Gap ranking and remediation

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Gaps are ranked by **structural consequence**, not by effort to fix: unfettered-decision gaps first, gaps in the record second, disclosure gaps third
- [ ] No gap was promoted because it is easy to close, or demoted because it is hard
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Every gap names a **concrete closing instrument** — an actual document or decision — plus a named owner and a date
- [ ] No remediation is an exhortation. "Strengthen independence", "improve governance", "be more rigorous" do not appear as remediations
- [ ] Findings are **structural, not personal**: the missing instrument is reported, not the individual — unless the record shows a known limit was deliberately bypassed, in which case that is stated plainly

## 6. Attribution

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Every recommendation is marked `CADBURY` (provision named), `DERIVED`, `CONSTRUCTION`, or `UNSOURCED`
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** No general governance practice is presented as a Code provision
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** No citation, title, year, author or provision has been invented. Where the exact wording of a provision was uncertain, the artifact describes the principle and instructs the reader to consult the source
- [ ] Anything untraceable is marked `UNSOURCED` rather than attributed to a plausible-sounding instrument
- [ ] The Cadbury Report is cited correctly: *Report of the Committee on the Financial Aspects of Corporate Governance*, United Kingdom, **December 1992**, together with its **Code of Best Practice**; committee chaired by Sir Adrian Cadbury
- [ ] Successor instruments (Greenbury 1995, Hampel 1998, the Combined Code 1998, Turnbull 1999, Higgs 2003, Smith 2003, the UK Corporate Governance Code from 2010, the OECD Principles) are named for **orientation only** — none of their provisions is paraphrased as though verified, and none is cited from memory
- [ ] Frameworks owned by sibling agents are not appropriated: COSO *Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance* (2017) belongs to risk-oversight; COSO *Internal Control — Integrated Framework* (1992, updated 2013) and the audit-committee material belong to audit-lead; *Boards That Lead* (Charan, Carey & Useem, 2013) belongs to succession-lead

## 7. Compliance claims

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** No compliance claim appears that the examined artifacts support. An `ASSERTED` provision is reported as a **departure** until the artifact exists
- [ ] In a comply-or-explain statement, every `DEPARTED` provision states three things: what the provision requires, why the organization departs, and the compensating safeguard adopted
- [ ] A departure with no safeguard is reported as **"a departure with no safeguard"**, in those words
- [ ] The artifact states that a comply-or-explain statement is a **governance disclosure exercise** carrying no view on any legal or listing obligation

## 8. Professional limit — mandatory, no exceptions

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The professional-limit block appears verbatim in the artifact
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact states that it is **not legal advice, not a statutory-audit opinion, not a listing-rule determination and not a compliance certificate**, and that **no audit opinion of any kind is issued**
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact states that it is **input for review by the directors and by licensed professionals**, never the final governance opinion
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Every statutory, listing-rule, contractual, tax, statutory-audit or employment-law question is listed as REFERRED OUT — none answered, none approximated, none "read as likely"
- [ ] A duty-check output states explicitly that it assesses **process and record in the governance sense**, and is not a legal opinion on duty, breach, liability or consequence
- [ ] The human-review clause closes the artifact
- [ ] No sentence would read, to a non-specialist, as a legal conclusion or a compliance certification

## 9. Record

- [ ] The audit is written to a versioned file under `squads/board/data/` — a governance finding that exists only in a transcript was never made
- [ ] The date and the artifacts examined are recorded by path
- [ ] Nothing produced crosses into risk appetite design, control evidence, succession assessment, epic framing, story drafting, implementation, testing or release

---

## Disposition

| Result | Condition | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **ISSUE** | Every blocking item satisfied; any remaining gap named in the artifact itself | Write to `squads/board/data/`, hand to board-chief for agenda placement |
| **REWORK** | A non-blocking gap that changes what a reader would conclude | Fix and re-run sections 3, 5 and 6 |
| **HOLD** | Any blocking item unsatisfied | Do not issue. The most common cause is a `PRESENT` mark that never met the operated test |
