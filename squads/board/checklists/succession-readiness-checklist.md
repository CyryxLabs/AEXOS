# Succession Readiness Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BRD-CL-005
**Referenced by:** succession-lead
**Applied to:** any succession audit, criteria set, bench review, candidate assessment, emergency
succession plan, key-person map, transition plan or executive assessment before it reaches the board
**Purpose:** Enforce the distinctions the method turns on — **a plan that exists versus a plan that
functions**, **criteria derived from strategy versus from the incumbent**, **evidence observed
versus evidence reported**, **a bench versus a list of talented people** — and stop any artifact
that characterizes a person rather than assessing against criteria.

## 1. Mode

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The board's mode for this matter is declared **before** any content: TAKE CHARGE / PARTNER / STAY OUT OF THE WAY
- [ ] Chief-executive succession, chief-executive assessment and the criteria for both are marked TAKE CHARGE without re-deriving it each time
- [ ] Where the mode is PARTNER, the artifact states **precisely what the board contributes and what management contributes** — unspecified partnering decays into one of the other modes by drift
- [ ] The consequence of getting the mode wrong is stated **in both directions** — under-involvement and over-involvement both have costs, and naming both stops the mode decision from becoming a proxy for how assertive the board feels
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The honest current-state question is answered in writing: is the board operating in take-charge mode, or has it **delegated the process to the incumbent while retaining the appearance of ownership**?
- [ ] The mode is recorded with the matter, so it can be checked later. Modes drift silently

## 2. Criteria — derived from strategy, not from the incumbent

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Criteria are derived from **the strategy** — what the organization will be doing in three to five years that it is not doing now — not from the role description and not from the incumbent
- [ ] Criteria are written as **capabilities with evidence**, not as attributes. "Visionary", "strategic", "strong leader" do not appear as criteria
- [ ] Criteria are separated into MUST / STRONGLY PREFERRED / NICE. A list where everything is essential has not been prioritized and will be applied inconsistently
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The **incumbent-contamination test** was applied in **both directions**:
      forward — would this criterion be here if the current chief executive were someone else?
      **inverse** — does the list **omit** anything the incumbent is weak at that the next phase requires?
- [ ] The inverse direction has a written answer, not a note that it was considered. It is nearly invisible and usually the more consequential of the two
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Criteria were **agreed by the board before any name was discussed**, and both dates are recorded. Criteria agreed after names are in the room are criteria shaped by the names
- [ ] The review trigger is a **change in strategy**, not the calendar
- [ ] The contamination test is marked `CONSTRUCTION`

## 3. Bench — the distinction between a bench and a list of talented people

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Agreed criteria exist. A bench review without criteria produces a popularity ranking, and the artifact stopped rather than producing one
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Every candidate carries a **readiness horizon**: READY NOW / READY 1–2 / READY 3+ / NOT ON THIS PATH / UNVERIFIED. **"Talented" is not a horizon** and was not accepted as an answer
- [ ] `UNVERIFIED` is used where someone is well regarded with no observed evidence against criteria, and the artifact states that **this is not a rating**
- [ ] Per candidate and per criterion, the artifact records **evidence observed, evidence absent, and the rating basis**
- [ ] Evidence types are distinguished: `DELIVERED` / `OBSERVED` / `REPORTED` / `INFERRED` — and `INFERRED` is treated as a **gap**, not a rating
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** For everyone not ready now, the artifact states **precisely what would close the gap** — which experience, which exposure, which stretch assignment, on what timescale. A horizon with no development attached is a prediction, not a plan
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The **bench-of-one rule** was applied without exception: where exactly one internal candidate is named for a role, the artifact states that **there is no internal succession for that role, plus one person who might work out**. The alternative reads as coverage
- [ ] The **observation gap** is stated as a finding wherever the board has never directly observed a candidate — it is closed by exposure to the board over time, not by a longer interview
- [ ] Anyone on the bench three or more years without moving is named, with the diagnosis: development failure, retention risk, or a rating that was never honest
- [ ] The **pipeline check** was applied: what change in skills, time application and values does the next level require, and is the candidate showing any of it yet? The most common failure is excellence at the previous level
- [ ] The bench-of-one rule and the observation-gap rule are marked `CONSTRUCTION`

## 4. Emergency succession — exists versus functions

**A plan that exists is not a plan that functions.** This is where the distinction bites hardest,
because the gap only becomes visible on the morning it matters.

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact asks not only whether a written emergency plan **exists**, but whether it **currently functions**
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The **authority instrument** is named, and it is confirmed **passed**. Where the plan depends on a board resolution that has not been passed, the artifact states in those words that **the plan does not currently function**, and the resolution is put forward now
- [ ] It is confirmed that the named interim would actually be **available and willing** — not assumed from the org chart
- [ ] The date anyone last **walked through** the first 48 hours is recorded. A plan reviewed but never rehearsed is marked as untested
- [ ] The **first-48-hours sequence** is written: who is told, in what order, by whom — staff, customers, suppliers, funders, any regulator. Sequence matters more than content and is almost never written down
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The **interim mandate boundary** is written explicitly: what the interim may decide and what they may not. An interim with the full mandate becomes a candidate by incumbency; an interim with no mandate cannot operate
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** It is stated explicitly whether **the interim is or is not a candidate** for the permanent role. Ambiguity there distorts everyone's behaviour immediately
- [ ] Coordination with governance-counsel on the authority instrument is recorded

## 5. Key person and concentration

- [ ] Single points of knowledge, authority and relationship are enumerated, **including the founder**
- [ ] Each carries a time to cover and a statement of what is documented anywhere
- [ ] The concentration is handed to **risk-oversight** for the risk portfolio, with the readiness assessment retained here

## 6. Internal versus external

- [ ] The framing as a **preference** was refused. The question is structured on the four variables: continuity value, change mandate, bench reality, integration risk
- [ ] **Bench reality** is honestly rated — the artifact states where the internal option is really a development plan
- [ ] The **asymmetry** is stated explicitly: internal appointments fail slowly and visibly, external appointments fail fast and expensively; neither is an argument on its own, and both are usually the whole of the unstated argument in the room
- [ ] The **honest default** is stated where it applies: where the change mandate is high and the bench is not ready against criteria, external is the indicated answer — and the correct response to disliking that is to fix the bench years earlier, not to relax the criteria now
- [ ] The recommendation names **the variable that decided it** and **what would change the answer**

## 7. Assessment discipline — criteria only, never characterization

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** Assessment is **against the agreed criteria only**. No assessment of the person outside those criteria
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** No personality characterization, no psychological judgement, no psychometric inference appears anywhere in the artifact
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** In executive assessment, **results, capability for the next phase, and conduct are assessed and reported separately** — never netted into a single verdict. A strong results strand does not offset a conduct finding, and netting them is how conduct findings disappear
- [ ] Gaps are stated plainly, each with the development that would close it and a timescale
- [ ] What the assessment does **not** cover is stated explicitly: cultural-fit judgements not written into the criteria, and anything requiring employment-law input
- [ ] Where figures inform the assessment, the artifact states which, and whether they carry assurance — challenging them is `audit-lead`'s question, not this one's
- [ ] The three-strand separation is marked `CONSTRUCTION`

## 8. Cadence

- [ ] The artifact records when succession was last on the agenda as a **decision item rather than an update**
- [ ] The number of consecutive periods it has been deferred is recorded
- [ ] It is recorded whether the board has discussed **development** — what would move a named candidate from ready-in-two-years to ready-now. If succession is reviewed annually and development never is, the board is reviewing a fact it has chosen not to influence
- [ ] Findings are ranked by **what would hurt most if a departure happened this quarter**, not by ease of remedy

## 9. Professional limit — mandatory, no exceptions

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The professional-limit block appears verbatim in the artifact
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact states that **employment law, contract terms, notice, severance, compensation regulation, discrimination and immigration are outside it entirely** and go to qualified counsel before any action is taken
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact states that it is **not a psychological, psychometric or personality assessment**, and that no contributing agent is qualified to produce one
- [ ] The artifact states that it issues **no audit opinion of any kind** on any figure used within it
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The artifact states that it is **input for review by the directors and by licensed professionals**, and that every appointment, removal and transition decision is taken by humans who remain wholly accountable for it
- [ ] Confidentiality is stated, and the circulation list was decided by the board **before** the artifact was written — it names individuals and their assessed gaps
- [ ] The human-review clause closes the artifact
- [ ] No sentence would read, to a non-specialist, as an employment-law conclusion or a clinical assessment

## 10. Attribution

- [ ] **BLOCKING.** *Boards That Lead* is cited correctly and completely: **Ram Charan, Dennis Carey and Michael Useem**, *Boards That Lead: When to Take Charge, When to Partner, and When to Stay Out of the Way*, Harvard Business Review Press, **2013**
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** *The Leadership Pipeline* is kept **entirely separate**: **Ram Charan, Stephen Drotter and James Noel**, **2001** — a **different book with different co-authors**, used only for level-transition reasoning. Neither work is ever cited for the other's content, and the two attributions are never merged
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** The **honest qualification** appears: *Boards That Lead* is a book about board leadership across many matters, of which succession is one — **it is not a step-by-step succession manual**
- [ ] Emergency plan structure, readiness horizons, transition sequencing and the bench-of-one rule are marked `DERIVED` (general board practice) or `CONSTRUCTION` — **never presented as the authors' text**
- [ ] **BLOCKING.** No provision, title, year or author has been invented. Where the precise wording of either work matters to a decision, the artifact directs the reader to the source
- [ ] Sibling frameworks are not appropriated: Cadbury (1992) belongs to governance-counsel; COSO ERM (2017) belongs to risk-oversight; COSO *Internal Control* (1992/2013), Sarbanes-Oxley (2002) and the three-lines model belong to audit-lead

## 11. Boundary

- [ ] Nothing here sets board composition rules, independence criteria or appointment authority — `governance-counsel`
- [ ] Nothing here designs enterprise risk appetite — `risk-oversight`; key-person concentration is **named** here and **owned** there
- [ ] Nothing here asserts that a figure or a control is reliable — `audit-lead`
- [ ] Nothing here executes recruitment, interviewing or sourcing — management
- [ ] Nothing here manages individual performance below the executive team — management
- [ ] Nothing drafts an epic (`@pm`), a story (`@sm`), an implementation (`@dev`), a test (`@qa`) or a release (`@devops`)
