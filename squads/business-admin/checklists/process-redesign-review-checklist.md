# Process Redesign Review Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BA-CL-004
**Referenced by:** `process-lead` (Sluice)
**Applies to:** any end-to-end map, handoff inventory, value test, control audit, resequencing,
capture-once design, case-owner design, automation verdict or full redesign proposal — before it is
captured, circulated or implemented.

> **PROFESSIONAL LIMIT.** This checklist tests whether a **process design** is honest and whether it
> stayed inside its boundary. It cannot test whether anything is compliant, whether a control may be
> removed, whether a retention period is right, or whether a role change is lawful. Nobody in this
> squad may assess any of those. Passing every item below authorises nothing — only the named owners
> in Section A do that.
>
> Framework applied with attribution: Hammer & Champy, *Reengineering the Corporation* (1993);
> Hammer, "Reengineering Work: Don't Automate, Obliterate", *HBR*, July–August 1990. Applied with
> its documented failure record attached (Section G).

[[LLM: INITIALIZATION — PROCESS REDESIGN REVIEW

1. Section Z first. If it trips, stop and route. No partial assessment.
2. Section A is BLOCKING.
3. Then the applicable sections.
4. Every failure gets a specific fix naming the step, handoff or control concerned.

This checklist reads and reports. It removes nothing and approves nothing.]]

## Section Z — Stop test. Run first.

- [ ] Does the work require deciding whether a **control may be removed, weakened or retimed**?
      → **STOP.** Propose with evidence to the named owner. Never decide.
- [ ] Does it touch **segregation of duties**? → **STOP. Excluded from this analysis entirely.**
      It exists to prevent something a cycle-time study cannot see.
- [ ] Does it require deciding **what records must be kept, for how long, or in what form**?
      → **STOP. Counsel.**
- [ ] Does it change **what personal data is captured, moved, merged, retained or who can access
      it**? → **STOP. Counsel, before the design proceeds.**
- [ ] Does it change **what people do, how many are needed, or where work is performed**?
      → **STOP. Qualified HR and employment counsel, at the point the consequence appears.**
- [ ] Does it require stating that something is **compliant or lawful**? → **STOP.**
- [ ] **Is the actual objective headcount reduction?** → **STOP and say so plainly.** That is an HR
      and employment-counsel matter and must not be run as a process programme.
- [ ] Unclear which side this sits on? → **Licensed territory. STOP.** A process improvement that
      quietly removed a control is the most expensive thing this role can produce.

## Section A — Blocking. Any unchecked item stops implementation.

- [ ] **A1.** The professional-limit notice is at the **top** of the artefact, before the map.
- [ ] **A2.** The **required-review table** exists, names an owner per review (person and firm where
      external), and carries a status per review.
- [ ] **A3.** The review list is itemised at minimum: control changes; segregation of duties
      (marked excluded); records retention; data protection; role/headcount/location; financial
      effect.
- [ ] **A4.** The required-review section is **prominent and near the front** — never a footnote. A
      redesign that reads as approved and carries its constraints at the end will be implemented
      without them.
- [ ] **A5.** The proposed-design section is **marked as a proposal** at the section itself, not
      only in the preamble, while any blocking review is outstanding.
- [ ] **A6.** **No control is removed, weakened or retimed in the artefact.** Every control change is
      a routed proposal addressed to a named owner, containing the evidence and the question.
- [ ] **A7.** Segregation-of-duties controls are identified and explicitly **excluded** from the
      analysis.
- [ ] **A8.** The artefact states nowhere that any process, step or design is compliant or lawful.
- [ ] **A9.** Every measurement is marked **OBSERVED**, **RECORDED** or **ESTIMATED** (Constitution
      Article IV). No estimate is presented as a measurement.
- [ ] **A10.** Every expected effect is **derived from mapped figures** or explicitly marked as an
      estimate. No number without a derivation.

---

## Section B — Process definition and boundary

- [ ] **B1.** The process is defined by its **output and its customer**, not by the department that
      hosts most of it.
- [ ] **B2.** It can be stated as "X requests Y and receives Z". If not, the boundary is wrong.
- [ ] **B3.** The **true start** is the moment the customer first wanted something — usually earlier
      than the hosting department believes.
- [ ] **B4.** The **true end** is the moment they could use the output — usually later.
- [ ] **B5.** Every function the process crosses is in the map, including functions outside the
      sponsor's organisation. A map that stops at a department boundary hides the seams.
- [ ] **B6.** The map was walked **with the people who perform the work**, not assembled from
      documentation. A redesign built without them is a diagram.

## Section C — Measurement

- [ ] **C1.** Per step: performer, system, working time, elapsed time from arrival to completion,
      volume per period, rework rate.
- [ ] **C2.** **Elapsed time and working time are measured separately** and the ratio is stated
      prominently. It is usually the headline finding.
- [ ] **C3.** Undocumented steps are recorded **separately**, with who performs them, who added them
      and what went wrong that caused them.
- [ ] **C4.** No undocumented step has been deleted for being undocumented. Each is investigated —
      they are usually checks somebody added after an incident, and each has an owner to find.
- [ ] **C5.** **No proposals appear in the mapping section.** A map that arrives with
      recommendations attached stops being challenged.
- [ ] **C6.** Sources are named for RECORDED figures; the observer and date are named for OBSERVED
      figures; the estimator is named for ESTIMATED figures.

## Section D — Handoffs and queues

- [ ] **D1.** Every point where work changes hands is listed — person, team, function, system,
      organisation.
- [ ] **D2.** Wait is measured and **split into three types**: waiting for capacity, waiting for a
      scheduled event, waiting for a decision. They have different fixes and are conflated
      constantly.
- [ ] **D3.** What is **lost** at each handoff is recorded: context, intent, urgency, and any
      information re-entered rather than carried.
- [ ] **D4.** Handoffs existing only because of the organisation chart are identified as such.
- [ ] **D5.** **Round trips are counted** and their rate reported. Work returning to a previous
      holder is a defect signal, and the rate matters more than the duration.
- [ ] **D6.** Handoffs are ranked **twice** — by elapsed time contributed and by error contribution.
      The rankings usually differ and both matter.

## Section E — Value test

- [ ] **E1.** Every step is classified into **exactly one** class: A serves the outcome; B produces
      genuinely required evidence; C prevents a known documented failure; D compensates for a defect
      elsewhere.
- [ ] **E2.** For every class B step, the requirer is **named specifically**. "Compliance" as an
      unnamed abstraction is not a source; if nobody can be named, the step is unclassified and goes
      to counsel as a question.
- [ ] **E3.** For every class C step, the failure is named **and the date it last occurred**. A step
      preventing a failure that has never occurred and cannot be described is a class D candidate.
- [ ] **E4.** Every class D step is traced to the specific upstream defect it compensates for.
- [ ] **E5.** **Class D share of total working time is quantified.** That figure is the argument for
      the redesign.
- [ ] **E6.** **Nothing is deleted at the value-test stage.** Classes B and C are routed to their
      named owners for confirmation.

## Section F — Controls

- [ ] **F1.** Every check, approval, sign-off and reconciliation is inventoried.
- [ ] **F2.** For each: named owner, why introduced, what it is intended to catch, how often it has
      caught it, and its cost in elapsed and working time.
- [ ] **F3.** Approvals with no observed rejection over a meaningful volume are flagged **as a
      question, not a conclusion**. An approval may deter rather than reject, and deterrence does
      not appear in a rejection count.
- [ ] **F4.** Every control proposal is addressed to a named owner, contains the evidence, and is
      **phrased as a question** — never as a recommendation framed as a decision.
- [ ] **F5.** Reconciliations are traced to the re-entry that causes them. Reconciliation is a
      symptom: it exists because the same information was captured more than once and the copies
      disagree.
- [ ] **F6.** Where an approval is proposed for removal, **what replaces its visibility is stated**.
      Removing an approval without replacing its visibility is how a control disappears unnoticed.
- [ ] **F7.** Any resequencing that changes **when** a control operates is flagged and routed to the
      control owner before it is proposed.

## Section G — Radical versus incremental, and method honesty

- [ ] **G1.** The required outcome is stated **numerically** — a target, not a direction.
- [ ] **G2.** The **incremental ceiling** is computed: what is reachable by removing waits,
      parallelising, and fixing the largest class D items within the current design.
- [ ] **G3.** If the ceiling reaches the target, **incremental is recommended plainly** — including
      when a radical programme has already been proposed and has sponsors.
- [ ] **G4.** If it does not, the **specific structural blocker** is named: fragmentation across
      functions, an information architecture forcing re-entry, a control model requiring sequential
      approval, or another named feature.
- [ ] **G5.** The honest cost of radical is stated: disruption, temporary performance loss during
      migration, the external time the professional reviews consume.
- [ ] **G6.** **The method's documented record is stated when radical is recommended**: the authors
      were explicit that a large share of reengineering efforts fail to achieve their intended
      results, and through the 1990s the label was widely used as cover for headcount reduction,
      damaging both the method's standing and the participation it requires.
- [ ] **G7.** The **motive check** was run and its answer recorded. If the objective is headcount
      reduction, the artefact says so plainly and routes it rather than laundering it.
- [ ] **G8.** The default is incremental, and any departure from it carries a written justification.

## Section H — Redesign content

- [ ] **H1.** The current design is presented with sourced measurements. No proposal is credible
      without it.
- [ ] **H2.** Every proposed change traces to **a specific design principle and a specific finding**
      from the map.
- [ ] **H3.** Principles are cited with attribution, and the 1990 article and the 1993 book are
      distinguished rather than merged.
- [ ] **H4.** Genuine dependencies were derived before resequencing; steps parallelised are those
      with no dependency between them, not those that felt independent.
- [ ] **H5.** Parallelisation does not create a new integration problem at the end — parallel work is
      linked as it proceeds rather than merged afterwards.
- [ ] **H6.** For decision-point changes, the **blocker** is identified specifically: information,
      authority, capability, or a control requirement. Where it is a control requirement, it is
      routed rather than designed around.
- [ ] **H7.** Capture-once designs trace each data item from origin through every re-entry, name the
      authoritative source, and attribute each reconciliation to the specific re-entry that causes
      it.
- [ ] **H8.** **Capture-once designs are routed to counsel before proposal** where they widen access
      to personal data, merge data sets, or change what is retained — a capture-once design
      frequently does all three.
- [ ] **H9.** Case-owner designs state the authority scope, the information access required, and the
      escalation path — and the escalation does not become a handoff that recreates the original
      problem.
- [ ] **H10.** Any effort or cash release counts only reconciliations **whose owners have confirmed
      they may go**.

## Section I — Automation gating

- [ ] **I1.** The value test was run **before** any tool was evaluated.
- [ ] **I2.** The question "would this process exist if designed today from the outcome backwards?"
      is asked and answered.
- [ ] **I3.** What automating the current design would **lock in** is stated: the current handoffs,
      sequence, data model, and the reconciliations that exist because of re-entry.
- [ ] **I4.** The design is produced **first**, then the automation question. This is the order the
      source insists on and the order almost always reversed in practice.
- [ ] **I5.** The **reversal cost** is stated. Automated processes are markedly harder to change than
      manual ones, which is precisely why the design must be right first.
- [ ] **I6.** Where the process should not exist, the artefact says so plainly — and expects
      resistance, because automation proposals usually arrive with a sponsor and a budget already
      attached.

## Section J — Risk, migration, capture

- [ ] **J1.** Risks include **what could get worse**: new failure modes, capability gaps, temporary
      performance loss during migration.
- [ ] **J2.** A migration path states how the process runs during the transition.
- [ ] **J3.** A **reversal plan** exists with a named decider.
- [ ] **J4.** Success measures are the same measures as the map, re-taken, with a date and a named
      checker.
- [ ] **J5.** Versioned file under `docs/`, with date, named owner and version.
- [ ] **J6.** **Both the current and the proposed design are preserved** in version control so the
      change stays auditable.

## Scoring

**Stop gate:** Section Z clean. If any item trips, verdict is **ROUTED** and no score is produced.

**Blocking gate:** all of Section A checked. Any unchecked item gives verdict
**NOT IMPLEMENTABLE**, regardless of score.

**Score:** (checked items in B–J) / (applicable items in B–J) × 100

| Grade | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90–100% | Implementable once the named reviews return |
| B | 80–89% | Implementable with the listed fixes applied first |
| C | 70–79% | The map is not yet good enough to argue from. Remeasure. |
| D | 60–69% | Substantial rework |
| F | <60% | Do not circulate. A redesign proposed from a map of beliefs is a rewrite with extra confidence. |

## Fix priority

1. **Section Z, Section A** — the boundary, the routed control proposals, the review table's
   prominence. Conditions of existence, not quality.
2. **C2 — elapsed versus working time.** Without it the conversation goes to effort, which is the
   wrong conversation and the one most likely to blame people.
3. **E2, E3 — named requirers and dated failures.** Unnamed "compliance" requirements are how
   overhead becomes permanent, and undescribed failures are how it stays.
4. **F3, F6 — the deterrence question and the visibility replacement.** These are the two places a
   cycle-time analysis most easily destroys something valuable.
5. **G6, G7 — the method's record and the motive check.** Applying a framework honestly includes
   reporting how often it has not worked, and refusing to be its cover story.
6. Everything else.
