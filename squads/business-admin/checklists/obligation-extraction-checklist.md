# Obligation Extraction Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BA-CL-003
**Referenced by:** `legal-ops` (Codex)
**Applies to:** any obligation register, renewal calendar, contract register, counsel instruction
pack, signature-authority matrix, escalation rule or lifecycle map before it is captured or relied
on operationally.

> **BOUNDARY.** This checklist tests whether an **administrative extraction** stayed administrative.
> It cannot test whether an extraction is legally correct, because assessing that is the practice of
> law and nobody in this squad may do it. Passing every item below does not make the register legal
> advice, does not make it privileged, and does not make any entry a statement about what a clause
> means.
>
> **NOTHING IN THIS WORKFLOW IS PRIVILEGED.** Assume it is disclosable. If the matter is or could
> become contentious, this checklist is not what you need — stop documenting and route to counsel.
>
> Organising reference: the legal operations discipline, most widely organised through the CLOC core
> competency model — a professional-association framework, not a single-author work, revised since
> first publication. Read the current version from CLOC before citing it externally.

## Section Z — Stop test. Run first.

- [ ] Does the request, or anything discovered during extraction, require saying what a clause
      **means, permits, triggers, or is worth**? → **STOP. Qualified lawyer. Clean refusal, then
      offer to assemble the instruction pack.**
- [ ] Does it require assessing **enforceability, validity, compliance, lawfulness or risk**?
      → **STOP.**
- [ ] Is there any **dispute, claim, demand, threat, regulator or authority contact** in view?
      → **STOP. Raise the privilege warning. Freeze further documentation until counsel says how
      they want the matter recorded.**
- [ ] Would the output be read by a **court, regulator, authority or counterparty**? → **STOP.**
- [ ] Is the document **unexecuted, partially signed, or missing amendments**? → **STOP extracting.**
      Record it as incomplete. A register built from a draft will be trusted as though it were not.
- [ ] Is it unclear whether this is process or law? → **It is law. STOP.**

A stop is a complete refusal plus a written question for counsel and a list of what to attach. It is
never a general observation, never "what usually happens", and never routed to another squad agent
so it can be answered indirectly.

---

## Section A — Blocking. Any unchecked item stops operational use.

- [ ] **A1.** The boundary notice is at the **top** of the artefact: administrative extraction, not
      interpretation, not legal advice.
- [ ] **A2.** The **privilege notice** is present and states plainly that nothing here is
      privileged.
- [ ] **A3.** A counsel block exists naming a specific lawyer (person and firm), the jurisdictions,
      what was sent, when, and the status.
- [ ] **A4.** **Every entry carries a clause reference.** Entries without one have been *removed*,
      not estimated, not carried "provisionally".
- [ ] **A5.** **No entry characterises an obligation.** No "onerous", "standard", "favourable",
      "market", "low risk", "boilerplate". Each of those is a legal assessment.
- [ ] **A6.** No entry states, restates, paraphrases-into-meaning, or summarises what a clause does.
- [ ] **A7.** The artefact contains no summary or précis of the agreement. A précis is an
      interpretation.
- [ ] **A8.** An **AMBIGUOUS list exists as its own section**, is routed to counsel with a written
      question per item, and **none of its items appear in the register as facts** — including as
      provisional rows with a caveat.
- [ ] **A9.** No inferred date. Nothing filled in from what a similar contract usually says
      (Constitution Article IV).
- [ ] **A10.** Where counsel has answered, the response is **attached or referenced, never
      summarised**. A summary loses the qualifications and becomes the version people quote.

---

## Section B — Document identity

- [ ] **B1.** The document is confirmed executed and final, by all parties.
- [ ] **B2.** Parties recorded exactly as named on the document, not as the organisation calls them.
- [ ] **B3.** Execution date and stated effective date recorded separately — they differ more often
      than people expect.
- [ ] **B4.** The complete amendment set is identified and located. Amendments filed away from the
      original are a known and expensive failure mode.
- [ ] **B5.** Side letters, order forms, statements of work and annexes identified.
- [ ] **B6.** The authoritative document location is recorded and retrievable in seconds by someone
      other than the extractor.
- [ ] **B7.** The extractor is named and the extraction date recorded.

## Section C — Entry discipline

- [ ] **C1.** Every entry records what the document **states**, verbatim or closely paraphrased with
      no characterisation added.
- [ ] **C2.** Every entry has a **named individual owner**. "The company", "the team", "operations"
      and "legal" are not owners.
- [ ] **C3.** Every dated entry has a **lead time**.
- [ ] **C4.** Every dated entry has an **action date** = deadline − lead time, and the action date is
      treated as the real date.
- [ ] **C5.** Obligation types covered systematically, not opportunistically: renewal, expiry, notice
      period, deliverable, reporting duty, payment milestone, stated contact and notice address.
- [ ] **C6.** Durations are computed only from **stated** starts. A duration computed from an event
      whose occurrence is not determinable from the document is AMBIGUOUS, not an entry.
- [ ] **C7.** **Auto-renewal mechanics are flagged prominently** — they are the most common source of
      unintended commitment.
- [ ] **C8.** No entry owned by someone who has left the organisation.

## Section D — The AMBIGUOUS routing (the item most often skipped)

- [ ] **D1.** Every item where the date or duty depends on **interpreting** rather than **reading**
      is on the list.
- [ ] **D2.** Each item states why it cannot be extracted administratively, specifically — not "this
      is unclear".
- [ ] **D3.** Each item carries a question written in one sentence that counsel can answer directly.
- [ ] **D4.** Each item names the decision it blocks and the deadline for that decision.
- [ ] **D5.** The list has actually been **sent**, with a date, and its status is tracked.
- [ ] **D6.** No item was resolved by choosing the obvious reading, by adopting what similar
      contracts usually say, or by asking another agent.
- [ ] **D7.** Items outstanding past their deadline are flagged. An unanswered question blocking an
      initiative is a status nobody notices until it is late.

## Section E — Calendar and alerting

- [ ] **E1.** Every renewal, expiry and notice window from the register appears on the forward
      calendar with its clause reference.
- [ ] **E2.** **Two alerts per date**: one with enough time to run a decision, one before the window
      closes. A single alert on the deadline records the miss.
- [ ] **E3.** Each date states the **decision it forces** — renew, renegotiate, terminate, or do
      nothing deliberately.
- [ ] **E4.** The calendar is reconciled against the contract register on a stated cadence, with a
      named owner for the reconciliation.
- [ ] **E5.** The register does **not** advise whether to renew. Cost input is routed to
      `@business-admin:finance-lead`; anything about what the clause permits is counsel's.

## Section F — Register completeness

- [ ] **F1.** Entry into the register is a **condition of the lifecycle**, not an afterthought.
- [ ] **F2.** Completeness is verified by reconciliation against finance's supplier and customer
      records — that is how the agreements nobody registered are found.
- [ ] **F3.** The known failure modes are audited explicitly: executed outside the lifecycle; signed
      without authority; counterparty paper never registered; amendments separated from originals;
      documents held only by someone who has left.
- [ ] **F4.** Access is defined, and **the retention question is routed to counsel** rather than
      decided here. Retention periods are a legal and regulatory matter.

## Section G — Counsel instruction packs (where the artefact is a brief)

- [ ] **G1.** The specific question, in one sentence, and the business decision that depends on it.
- [ ] **G2.** The deadline, and what happens if it is missed.
- [ ] **G3.** A **factual** chronology with dates and sources — facts only, no characterisation, no
      inference, no legal framing.
- [ ] **G4.** Complete and current documents attached, including amendments and relevant
      correspondence.
- [ ] **G5.** Named business owner who can answer follow-ups.
- [ ] **G6.** Scope and budget expectation stated: what is asked and what explicitly is not.
- [ ] **G7.** **No legal analysis, no proposed position, no assessment of merit.** A brief that
      pre-frames the answer either wastes the instruction or corrupts it.
- [ ] **G8.** Where the matter is contentious, the pack notes that the operational record is
      unprivileged and asks counsel how they want it documented from here.

## Section H — Escalation rule and authority matrix (where applicable)

- [ ] **H1.** The escalation rule states the default first: **escalate**. It enumerates what may
      proceed *without* counsel, never what must reach them.
- [ ] **H2.** The rule has been **reviewed and approved by counsel**, with a date. Operations
      proposes the structure; counsel decides where the line sits. Not optional.
- [ ] **H3.** Hard triggers listed and unconditional: any dispute, threat, claim or demand; any
      regulator, authority or tax contact; counterparty paper; any deviation from an approved
      template; personal data, employment or safety; new jurisdiction or entity type; anything
      unfamiliar to the triager.
- [ ] **H4.** Every "proceeds without counsel" decision is recorded with its basis, so the rule can
      be audited later.
- [ ] **H5.** A review cadence with counsel is set, and any near-miss triggers an early review.
- [ ] **H6.** An authority matrix states explicitly that it is an **internal control design** and
      that whether a delegation is legally effective is a question for counsel.
- [ ] **H7.** Template custody records that **counsel authors and changes legal content**;
      operations controls versioning, distribution and deviation detection, and never edits legal
      content — including formatting changes that alter meaning.

## Section I — Capture

- [ ] **I1.** Versioned file under `docs/` with date, named owner and version.
- [ ] **I2.** Marks what it supersedes; superseded versions retired actively so an old register
      cannot be used by accident.
- [ ] **I3.** Open counsel questions listed separately and prominently, never as a footnote.
- [ ] **I4.** A review date and a reconciliation cadence are set.

---

## Scoring

**Stop gate:** Section Z clean. If any item trips, verdict is **ROUTED TO COUNSEL** and no score is
produced.

**Blocking gate:** all of Section A checked. Any unchecked Section A item gives verdict
**NOT USABLE**, regardless of score.

**Score:** (checked items in B–I) / (applicable items in B–I) × 100

| Grade | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90–100% | Operationally reliable. Dates will be caught. |
| B | 80–89% | Usable; fix the listed gaps within the cycle |
| C | 70–79% | Incomplete. Assume at least one date is not being watched. |
| D | 60–69% | Rework before anyone relies on it as a calendar |
| F | <60% | Do not rely on it. A register that looks complete and is not is worse than none, because it stops anyone looking. |

## Fix priority

1. **Section Z, Section A** — the boundary, the privilege warning, the clause references and the
   AMBIGUOUS routing. These are the conditions of the artefact's existence, not its quality.
2. **C2 — owners.** An obligation owned by nobody surfaces late, and the register looks complete the
   entire time.
3. **C7 and E2 — auto-renewal and two-stage alerts.** Missed dates are the most common avoidable
   legal cost, and both of these are pure administration.
4. **F2 — completeness reconciliation.** You cannot track what was never registered.
5. **H2 — counsel approval of the escalation rule.** An unapproved rule is operations deciding what
   needs a lawyer, which is itself a legal judgement.
6. Everything else.

## What this checklist does not do

It does not verify that any obligation was correctly identified as a matter of law, that the
register captures everything the agreement legally requires, or that the organisation is compliant
with anything. Those are questions for a qualified lawyer. This checklist verifies only that the
extraction stayed on the administrative side of a line that is very easy to cross by accident and
almost never crossed on purpose.
