# Financial Reading Quality Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BA-CL-001
**Referenced by:** `finance-lead` (Abacus)
**Applies to:** any profit-to-cash bridge, statement reading, ratio panel, cash-cycle measurement,
runway reading, variance read or return case before it is captured or used in a decision.

> **PROFESSIONAL LIMIT.** This checklist tests whether a *management reading* is good enough to
> carry an internal decision. It does not test whether books are correct, whether a treatment is
> right, or whether anything complies with a standard — none of which any agent in this squad may
> assess. Passing every item below does not make an output an audit, a review, a certification or
> anything an external party may rely on. Framework applied with attribution: Berman & Knight,
> *Financial Intelligence* (2006), a management-literacy framework that qualifies nobody to
> prepare, certify or opine on financial information.

[[LLM: INITIALIZATION — FINANCIAL READING QUALITY

Run this against the actual artefact, not against a memory of it. Open the file.

1. Mark [x] present and correct, [ ] missing or wrong, [N/A] genuinely not applicable.
2. Section A items are BLOCKING. A single unchecked Section A item stops the artefact from
   carrying a decision, regardless of the rest of the score.
3. Score Sections B-F for the grade.
4. Where an item fails, write the specific fix, not the general principle.

This checklist reads and reports. It changes no figure.]]

## Section A — Blocking. Any unchecked item stops the artefact.

- [ ] **A1.** The professional-limit notice is at the **top** of the artefact, before any figure —
      not in a closing caveat. (A caveat that arrives after the answer arrives after the answer has
      already been read as advice.)
- [ ] **A2.** No line of the output states or implies how any item should be recognised, measured,
      disclosed, classified or taxed.
- [ ] **A3.** No part of the output is presented as assurance, audit, review, certification, or
      material an external party may rely on.
- [ ] **A4.** A **licensed-review block** exists and names a specific professional (a person and a
      firm, not "our accountant"), the question sent, the date sent, and the review status.
- [ ] **A5.** Every licensed-professional question surfaced by the reading is written **as an
      answerable question** with the documents to attach listed, and appears near the front of the
      artefact rather than as a footnote.
- [ ] **A6.** Where the reading depends on an answer that has not returned, the dependency is
      stated at the point of the conclusion it affects — not only in the referral table.
- [ ] **A7.** No figure in a conclusion is without a traceable source (Constitution Article IV).
      Unsourced figures are quarantined and labelled, never smoothed.

---

## Section B — Scope and comparability

- [ ] **B1.** Entity or consolidation boundary stated before any figure.
- [ ] **B2.** Period stated as two explicit dates, not a month or quarter name.
- [ ] **B3.** Basis stated: accrual or cash, gross or net, what is included and excluded.
- [ ] **B4.** Currency stated, and any translation basis stated where more than one is involved.
- [ ] **B5.** Every comparison in the artefact passes the comparability gate: same period length,
      same entity boundary, same basis, same treatment of one-off items.
- [ ] **B6.** Where a comparison could not be made comparable, it is **absent**, not adjusted into
      shape with an unexplained factor.
- [ ] **B7.** Any comparison basis is named explicitly — own history, stated plan, or a documented
      external benchmark — and bases are not mixed inside one panel.

## Section C — Sourcing and the quality of each number

- [ ] **C1.** Every figure names its source: which system, which report or export, which extraction
      date. A slide is not a source; the export behind the slide is.
- [ ] **C2.** Figures are graded where a grade is meaningful: SOURCED AND STABLE, SOURCED BUT
      ASSUMPTION-SENSITIVE, or UNSOURCED. Only the first two carry a decision.
- [ ] **C3.** For each material figure, the estimates and judgements inside it are named — timing of
      recognition, allocation basis, provisions, capitalisation choices, manual adjustments.
- [ ] **C4.** Incentive exposure is noted where the preparer of a figure is measured on it. Named as
      structure, not as accusation.
- [ ] **C5.** A falsifier is stated for each load-bearing figure: what would have to be true for it
      to be materially wrong, and which check would reveal it.
- [ ] **C6.** No figure has been adjusted to make a total agree.

## Section D — The profit-to-cash test (the reading this squad exists to get right)

- [ ] **D1.** The relationship between profit and cash is **examined**, not assumed, for the period
      under discussion.
- [ ] **D2.** The bridge is built item by item, each with a named source and a class (non-cash,
      working capital, investing, financing, one-off).
- [ ] **D3.** One-off items are listed individually, not netted against each other.
- [ ] **D4.** The **unexplained residual is visible as its own line**, quantified, with what has and
      has not been checked, a named person tracing it, and a date.
- [ ] **D5.** The residual has not been absorbed into another line, relabelled as rounding without
      justification, or removed by adjusting a sourced figure.
- [ ] **D6.** The dominant driver is named and classified **structural or timing**, with the
      evidence that supports the classification rather than an assertion of it.
- [ ] **D7.** Where receivables dominate, growth is separated from execution: the receivables
      attributable to volume at constant days, and those attributable to the change in days.
- [ ] **D8.** Every working-capital release is presented as a **one-time release plus a permanently
      lower requirement at this volume** — never as recurring income and never inside a run rate.
- [ ] **D9.** Where a proposed lever shifts cost onto a counterparty (extending payment terms,
      slowing supplier payment), the commercial and relationship consequence is stated and the
      decision is marked as commercial rather than financial.

## Section E — Analysis discipline

- [ ] **E1.** Ratios are expressed as **investigation questions**, not verdicts. "Look here"
      rather than "act now".
- [ ] **E2.** Ratio inputs are shown, not only results, so the arithmetic can be checked.
- [ ] **E3.** Where a ratio sits outside expectation, the artefact states the operating causes worth
      checking rather than concluding from the ratio alone.
- [ ] **E4.** Cash conversion cycle, where computed, shows receivable days, inventory days and
      payable days separately with their inputs, and attributes the cycle to its dominant leg.
- [ ] **E5.** Runway, where stated, carries: the burn basis and what it includes and excludes;
      committed-but-unpaid obligations falling inside the horizon; at least two scenarios; the three
      variables that move the answer most; and the cash levels that force specific decisions.
- [ ] **E6.** No single runway figure appears anywhere without its assumptions attached.
- [ ] **E7.** Return cases state cash outflows and inflows **with timing**, label each flow as
      measured / estimated / assumed, and name the single load-bearing assumption whose failure
      alone kills the case, plus how to test it cheaply first.
- [ ] **E8.** Variances are decomposed into volume, price, mix and timing **before** anything is
      attributed to performance.
- [ ] **E9.** Movements caused by an estimate revision, reclassification or allocation change are
      labelled as such and are **not** reported as business events.
- [ ] **E10.** Costs classified as variable that are in practice fixed within the horizon are
      flagged. (This is the most common error in a downside scenario and it makes the downside look
      survivable when it is not.)

## Section F — Capture and traceability

- [ ] **F1.** Assumptions are in a dedicated section, each attributed to whoever supplied it, each
      with what would falsify it.
- [ ] **F2.** Conclusions are written separately from figures, so a reader can disagree with the
      conclusion and keep the data.
- [ ] **F3.** Open questions are listed, with which of them require a licensed professional.
- [ ] **F4.** The artefact is a versioned file under `docs/` with a date, a named owner and a
      version number.
- [ ] **F5.** Where it supersedes an earlier reading, the earlier artefact is referenced and the
      earlier one is marked superseded — a losing reading left unrevised in the repository will be
      cited again.
- [ ] **F6.** A review date is set.

---

## Scoring

**Blocking gate:** all of Section A checked. If any Section A item is unchecked, the verdict is
**NOT USABLE FOR A DECISION**, regardless of score, and the grade is not calculated.

**Score:** (checked items in B–F) / (applicable items in B–F) × 100

| Grade | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90–100% | Carries a material, hard-to-reverse decision |
| B | 80–89% | Carries a reversible decision; fix the gaps before the next reading |
| C | 70–79% | Useful as an internal discussion input; do not close a decision on it |
| D | 60–69% | Rework before circulation |
| F | <60% | Do not circulate. It will be quoted after it stops being true. |

## Fix priority

1. **Section A** — boundary, referral, sourcing. These are not quality issues; they are the
   conditions under which the artefact is allowed to exist.
2. **D4–D6** — the residual and the driver classification. A hidden residual is how a bridge
   becomes a story.
3. **B5–B7** — comparability. Most reporting disputes dissolve once this is enforced, and most
   confident wrong conclusions survive because it was not.
4. **E5–E7** — runway and return cases. These are the outputs that get quoted longest.
5. Everything else.

## What this checklist does not do

It does not verify that the underlying figures are correct. It verifies that the reading is honest
about where they came from, what is inside them, and what it does not know. Whether the books are
right is assurance work, performed by an auditor under a defined standard. Nothing here substitutes
for that and nothing here should be presented as though it did.
