# People Practice Review Checklist

**Checklist ID:** BA-CL-002
**Referenced by:** `people-lead` (Roster)
**Applies to:** any hiring standard, interview kit, work sample, decision process, calibration
design, conversation-separation design, pay framework, manager-effectiveness instrument, voice
survey, nudge or people experiment — **before** it reaches a candidate or an employee.

> **PROFESSIONAL LIMIT.** This checklist tests whether a practice **design** is sound. It does not
> and cannot test whether the practice is lawful. Nothing here is HR advice, employment advice or
> legal advice. Passing every item below does not make a practice deployable — only the completed,
> dated counsel review in Section A does that. Framework applied with attribution: Laszlo Bock,
> *Work Rules!* (2015), applied with the transfer caveats in Section G.

[[LLM: INITIALIZATION — PEOPLE PRACTICE REVIEW

## Section Z — Stop test. Run first, before anything else.

If **any** of these is true, stop. Do not assess the artefact, do not give a partial view, and do
not offer a general observation. Route it.

- [ ] Does the artefact, or the request to review it, concern a **named individual** — their
      performance, conduct, pay, grievance, discipline, dismissal, accommodation, investigation or
      improvement plan? → **STOP. Qualified HR and employment counsel. Entirely.**
- [ ] Does answering require **personal data** about an identifiable candidate or employee?
      → **STOP.** Convenience is not a lawful basis.
- [ ] Does the question turn on whether something is **lawful** in any jurisdiction?
      → **STOP. Employment counsel.**
- [ ] Would the output be read by a **tribunal, authority, regulator or opposing party**?
      → **STOP.**
- [ ] Is it unclear which side of the line this sits on? → **Treat as qualified territory. STOP.**

A stop is recorded with the question written for the professional and what to bring them. It is not
softened, not partially answered, and not routed to another squad agent so it can be answered
indirectly.

## Section A — Blocking. Any unchecked item stops deployment.

- [ ] **A1.** A **counsel-review block** exists, names a specific reviewer (person and firm), lists
      the jurisdictions covered, and carries a status.
- [ ] **A2.** The review status is **REVIEWED-APPROVED** or **REVIEWED-WITH-CHANGES with the changes
      applied**. UNREVIEWED and SENT-AWAITING mean the practice does not touch a person.
- [ ] **A3.** The counsel-review list is **itemised**, not a single line saying "reviewed by legal".
      At minimum: attributes and anchors, disqualifiers, question set, work-sample brief and IP
      position, candidate/employee data collected and retained, any automated scoring or third-party
      tooling, accommodation process, jurisdictional variation.
- [ ] **A4.** The professional-limit notice is at the **top** of the artefact, before any content.
- [ ] **A5.** No named individual appears anywhere in the artefact, including in examples.
- [ ] **A6.** No personal data appears anywhere in the artefact, including in illustrative rows.
- [ ] **A7.** The artefact states nowhere, in any framing, that a practice is lawful, compliant, or
      "generally fine".
- [ ] **A8.** Every claim about what works traces to the cited framework, to an internal
      measurement, or to a stated assumption. Unsupported folklore is marked UNVERIFIED and is out
      of the recommendation (Constitution Article IV).
- [ ] **A9.** **No validity coefficient, effect size or research statistic is quoted from memory.**
      Only the direction of a cited finding is reproduced. Where a number is genuinely needed, the
      artefact points to the underlying research instead of asserting a figure.

## Section B — Hiring bar and attributes

- [ ] **B1.** First-year outcomes were written **before** attributes were chosen, and every
      attribute traces to at least one outcome.
- [ ] **B2.** Outcomes state what is produced, not what is attended, owned or supported.
- [ ] **B3.** Five assessed attributes or fewer.
- [ ] **B4.** Every attribute has behavioural anchors at three or four levels, written in
      **observable** terms. "Lacks depth" is not an anchor.
- [ ] **B5.** Every attribute is owned by exactly one named interviewer.
- [ ] **B6.** Every attribute that cannot be evidenced in an interview or work sample has been
      removed, and the removal is recorded with its reason.
- [ ] **B7.** No undefined attribute ("fit", "polish", "hunger") survives. These are channels for
      similarity bias, which is a quality problem and a legal exposure at the same time.
- [ ] **B8.** Disqualifiers are listed **separately** from scored attributes, are objective and
      observable, and are individually flagged for counsel.

## Section C — Interview and work sample structure

- [ ] **C1.** Fixed questions per attribute, asked in the same order to every candidate.
- [ ] **C2.** Past-behaviour questions with follow-up probes are preferred over hypotheticals.
- [ ] **C3.** The rubric was written **before** any candidate was seen, and is attached.
- [ ] **C4.** The recording format is evidence → level → reasoning, in that order.
- [ ] **C5.** Independent submission is required and enforced: no interviewer discusses a candidate
      with another before submitting.
- [ ] **C6.** Every round is justified by what it adds that the previous round could not.
- [ ] **C7.** Work sample is a representative slice of actual work, time-bounded, and compensated if
      substantial.
- [ ] **C8.** Work sample does not depend on internal context the candidate cannot have.
- [ ] **C9.** Work-sample rubric written before the first submission; scoring blind where the format
      allows.
- [ ] **C10.** **Candidate output is never used as production work.** Stated explicitly in the
      artefact.

## Section D — Decision rights

- [ ] **D1.** The decision is separated from the hiring manager. A named deciding body or
      independent reviewer holds it.
- [ ] **D2.** The packet is defined: the bar, evidence per attribute, work sample and score,
      recommendation with reasoning.
- [ ] **D3.** The decision rule is written **in advance** — what evidence pattern is a hire, what is
      a no.
- [ ] **D4.** The escalation route for genuine disagreement is a decision, not a re-run of the loop
      until the desired answer appears.
- [ ] **D5.** The exception path exists, is written down, and is **visible** — lowering the bar is a
      recorded act, not a quiet one.
- [ ] **D6.** A retrospective loop exists: hires are revisited against the bar after a defined
      period to see whether the bar predicted anything.

## Section E — Packet quality (when reviewing a completed loop)

- [ ] **E1.** Every assessed attribute has recorded behavioural evidence, not adjectives.
- [ ] **E2.** The assigned rubric level matches the recorded evidence.
- [ ] **E3.** Submissions were independent — check timestamps and near-identical phrasing across
      interviewers, which is the usual tell.
- [ ] **E4.** The work sample was scored against the pre-written rubric, not against other
      candidates.
- [ ] **E5.** Packets where the recommendation is stronger than the evidence are flagged; so are
      packets where the evidence is stronger than the recommendation. Both are informative.
- [ ] **E6.** The review reports **evidentiary quality only**. It does not recommend a hiring
      outcome on an individual — that is the deciding body's, under a counsel-reviewed process.

## Section F — Performance, pay and organisation practice

- [ ] **F1.** Development conversations and rating/pay conversations are **separate**, with different
      names, cadences and owners.
- [ ] **F2.** What each conversation is **forbidden** to contain is written down.
- [ ] **F3.** A calendar gap exists so the development conversation is not read as the pay
      conversation's preamble.
- [ ] **F4.** Calibration runs on **pre-submitted written evidence** against the same criteria.
- [ ] **F5.** Calibration has a named resolution authority, not consensus decay.
- [ ] **F6.** Reasoning for every adjusted rating is recorded, so the person can be told something
      true afterwards.
- [ ] **F7.** Any distribution pattern that could indicate bias is **routed to counsel and qualified
      HR**, not analysed here.
- [ ] **F8.** Both tails are addressed as **system design** — a diagnostic sequence for the
      struggling tail, a study loop for the top tail — with a hard escalation line at the point
      individual consequences begin.
- [ ] **F9.** The struggling-tail diagnostic runs before any conclusion about a person: role clarity,
      placement, support present, manager effectiveness, standard communicated and consistently
      applied.
- [ ] **F10.** Pay framework states what is being rewarded and why, in plain language, before any
      number.
- [ ] **F11.** The pay explanation a person would actually receive is written out, and is not
      embarrassing to read aloud.
- [ ] **F12.** **No numbers for named individuals.** Structure and rationale only. Affordability is
      referred to `@business-admin:finance-lead`; neither agent decides compliance.
- [ ] **F13.** Manager upward feedback is kept **out of** the manager's own rating and compensation,
      with a minimum team size before any result is shown.
- [ ] **F14.** Any feedback disclosing possible misconduct, harassment or unlawful behaviour is
      routed immediately and **not processed, summarised or analysed here**.
- [ ] **F15.** A voice survey has a **committed action loop** — who reads, who responds, by when, how
      it is communicated — defined before the instrument. If not, the instrument is not designed.
- [ ] **F16.** Anonymity thresholds are stated to respondents in advance and honestly.
- [ ] **F17.** Any nudge is non-coercive and non-deceptive. A nudge that relies on people not
      noticing is a manipulation and it destroys the trust it was meant to build.

## Section G — Transfer honesty

- [ ] **G1.** Transfer caveats are stated: what about this design depends on organisation size,
      selectivity or budget that this organisation does not have.
- [ ] **G2.** The mechanism is distinguished from the implementation. Where the mechanism transfers
      and the implementation does not, the adaptation is written out.
- [ ] **G3.** No practice is recommended on the grounds that a well-known company uses it. The
      mechanism has been examined.
- [ ] **G4.** Pay differentiation carries the honest caveat that a wide spread is more defensible
      where there is volume of evidence than in a small team where one observation can drive a
      rating.

## Section H — Measurement and capture

- [ ] **H1.** Every practice carries an outcome measure.
- [ ] **H2.** A **pre-declared failure condition** exists, plus what happens on failure, including a
      commitment to reverse.
- [ ] **H3.** Confounds that could produce a false read are listed.
- [ ] **H4.** Any comparison that gives one group a worse experience carries an explicit decision and
      a counsel review — never a quiet rollout.
- [ ] **H5.** A practice that cannot be evaluated is not made mandatory.
- [ ] **H6.** The artefact is a versioned file under `docs/` with date, owner and version, and marks
      what it supersedes.

## Scoring

**Stop gate:** Section Z clean. If any Section Z item trips, the verdict is **ROUTED** and no score
is produced.

**Blocking gate:** all of Section A checked. If any Section A item is unchecked, the verdict is
**NOT DEPLOYABLE**, regardless of score.

**Score:** (checked items in B–H) / (applicable items in B–H) × 100

| Grade | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90–100% | Deployable once counsel review is complete |
| B | 80–89% | Deployable with the listed fixes applied first |
| C | 70–79% | Rework. The evidence it produces will not be reviewable by a non-attendee. |
| D | 60–69% | Substantial redesign |
| F | <60% | Do not deploy. A practice this loose applies a different standard to each person, which is the failure mode the design existed to prevent. |

## Fix priority

1. **Section Z and Section A** — these are not quality issues. They are the conditions under which
   the practice is permitted to exist.
2. **B7, B8** — undefined attributes and unreviewed disqualifiers. These are simultaneously the
   largest quality leak and the largest legal exposure.
3. **C3, C5** — rubric-before-candidates and independent submission. Without these the loop produces
   one shared impression wearing several signatures.
4. **D1, D5** — decision separation and the visible exception path. This is what holds the bar when
   the role has been open too long, which is exactly when it drifts.
5. **F1, F2** — conversation separation. When money is on the table nobody is learning; they are
   negotiating.
6. Everything else.
